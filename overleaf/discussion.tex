\section{Discussion}
This work adapts a diffeomorphic coarse-to-fine registration pipeline to longitudinal whole-body PSMA PET/CT, with an explicit focus on preserving PET-derived biomarkers.
Although checkpoint selection shows that registration accuracy alone is not sufficient for model selection, the final submitted configuration improves both alignment and PET preservation compared with the baselines.
Relative to the strongest baseline, it improves DSC and HD95 while reducing MTV and TLG bias, as well as non-diffeomorphic volume.
The biomarker terms are applied consistently during training and instance optimization, while the per-structure rigid fit constrains skeletal structures without relying on finite-difference stencils that may extend into surrounding soft tissue.

Several limitations qualify these results.
Evaluation relies on automatically generated surrogate labels, and the loss weights were selected on the same 20 cases used for comparison.
The reported results should therefore be interpreted as exploratory rather than confirmatory.
Hidden test-set results were unavailable at the time of writing.
The rigidity term also depends on the quality of the skeletal segmentations and is primarily intended for structures that are thin relative to the image resolution.

Finally, the 90s runtime constraint reduces the accuracy of the submitted container configuration, largely due to the segmentations required for instance optimization.
Future work will therefore focus on faster, more accurate segmentation and on evaluating biomarker preservation per lesion rather than only at the aggregate MTV and TLG levels.

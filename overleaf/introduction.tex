\section{Introduction}

Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) PET/CT has become a central imaging modality for staging, treatment planning, radiopharmaceutical therapy, and post-therapy response assessment in prostate cancer~\cite{ref_psma_clinical}.
In these settings, patients often undergo multiple scans over time, comprising a baseline scan before therapy and one or more follow-up scans.
Accurate baseline-to-follow-up registration can support comparison of disease burden, localisation of uptake changes, assessment of lesion progression or response, dose accumulation, and quantitative longitudinal analysis~\cite{ref_psmareg_challenge}.
The Learn2Reg~2026 PSMAReg challenge~\cite{ref_learn2reg_alessa,ref_learn2reg_hansen,ref_psmaregdata} formalises this problem as the estimation of a dense displacement field that warps each follow-up (moving) scan to its baseline (fixed) scan.
The evaluation is performed by a combination of an organ overlap metric, boundary distance and deformation regularity.
Moreover, to measure the preservation of the PET-derived tumor biomarkers, two separate metrics are used: the metabolic tumor volume (MTV) and the total lesion glycolysis (TLG).

The challenge organisers identify several factors that make establishing this correspondence by deformable image registration difficult in routine whole-body data~\cite{ref_psmareg_challenge}: baseline and follow-up scans differ substantially in field of view, patient positioning, respiratory state, disease burden, bladder and bowel filling, and body weight; CT and PET encode complementary but appearance-dissimilar information; and the registration must preserve the quantitative PET signal used for response assessment while remaining physically plausible in anatomical structures.

Deformable registration has traditionally been posed as an iterative optimisation over a regularised objective functional, with widely used methods including the diffeomorphic Demons~\cite{ref_demons}, SyN~\cite{ref_syn}, and B-spline~\cite{bspline} frameworks.
While accurate, these methods are slow at whole-body resolution.
Learning-based methods instead amortise the optimisation over a training set into the network weights, replacing per-pair iterative optimisation with an efficient single forward pass at inference.
VoxelMorph established the unsupervised paradigm~\cite{ref_voxelmorph_cvpr}, and subsequent work introduced diffeomorphic parameterisations through stationary velocity fields~\cite{ref_svf_voxelmorph}, transformer backbones such as TransMorph~\cite{ref_transmorph}, and multi-resolution architectures such as LapIRN~\cite{ref_lapirn}.
Among the latter, LapIRN uses a Laplacian pyramid of coarse-to-fine sub-networks to recover large deformations while keeping the transformation smooth and invertible.

The skeleton is the main location of PSMA ocurrences and thus, the accurate alignment of the bone structures is particular important. 
In LapIRN, global smoothness regularization is employed, which prevents local folding.
However, it is desired to constrain the bone structures to move rigidly.
Anatomy-aware regularisation that locally penalises non-rigid deformation of individual structures was widely explored~\cite{ref_rigidity_1_loeckx,ref_rigidity_2_ruan,ref_rigidity_3_staring,ref_rigidity_4_staring_modersitzki}.
The regularization is therefore particularly relevant for the registration problem of the challenge.

In this work, we extend LapIRN by addressing the challenges presented above for longitudinal whole-body PSMA PET/CT registration in the PSMAReg task (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}).


Our contributions are threefold.
We propose:
\begin{itemize}
  \item an adaptation of the diffeomorphic, coarse-to-fine LapIRN architecture to longitudinal whole-body PSMA PET/CT, combining an affine pre-registration with a three-level pyramid and
        test-time instance optimization;
  \item a set of losses that constrain the PET-derived MTV and TLG given the estimated deformation, including a Jacobian determinant-based loss term;
  \item a local rigidity regularization for the bone structures;
\end{itemize}

\caption{Overview of the proposed registration pipeline. The moving (follow-up) and fixed (baseline) PET/CT are first coarsely aligned by an affine pre-registration (red), and the affinely aligned pair is passed to the coarse-to-fine LapIRN network, which predicts a dense displacement field (DF, green grid) used to resample the moving scan into the fixed space (warped PET/CT).
Training is driven by three groups of objectives:
\textbf{(1) Regularisation} on the DF, a global smoothness and folding penalty together with a per-structure bone-rigidity term, which by keeping the skeleton rigid also preserves the biomarkers of the many lesions that are intraosseous;
\textbf{(2) PET preservation}, the MTV, TLG and lesion-Jacobian terms, comparing the moving and warped lesions (black dashed);
and \textbf{(3) NCC + DSC}, image- and label-based similarity between the fixed and warped scans (grey dashed).
At test time, the same objectives are descended per pair by instance optimisation (IO).}
\label{fig:pipeline}

\section{Results}

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We report our method in three configurations: \emph{ours (container)}, the submitted method of Sec.~\ref{sec:container}, which is what the hidden test set will be scored on and therefore the one we test the baselines against; \emph{ours (validation)}, the method of Sec.~\ref{sec:pipeline}; and \emph{ours (no IO)}, the backbone alone, which isolates what instance optimization contributes.
The latter two carry no significance marks.

\subsection{Ablation of the Objective}
\label{sec:ablation}

\subsubsection{Derivative-based rigidity is ill posed on thin bone.}
We quantified the stencil leakage of Sec.~\ref{sec:losses} across five cases: 53.0\% (range 51.6 to 54.7) of the voxels entering the loss were not bone, and 44.8\% of the gradient magnitude fell outside bone.
A field with zero displacement inside every bone, which is perfectly rigid by construction and should therefore cost nothing, scored 56 (range 40 to 69), all of which vanished after a single mask erosion.
The term was thus measuring the bone-to-soft tissue displacement discontinuity that any correct registration must exhibit.
With the per-structure rigid-fit penalty of Sec.~\ref{sec:losses}, the zero displacement field now scores exactly 0 and a per-bone rigid field 2.4e-6, while anisotropic squeeze is still rejected at 1.16.

\subsubsection{A surface-distance did not increase alignment accuracy.}
Since HD95 is scored, a surface-distance term~\cite{salcudean} is the obvious addition to the objective, but the $2296$ scored (case,\,label) entries leave it nothing to do.
Half the HD95 mass sits in the worst $10\%$ of entries, and splitting it against the unregistered pair shows that $48.1\%$ is inherent, already bad before registration and no better after, while none of it was created by our field.
Those entries are limbs repositioned between sessions, hollow organs with changing filling and anatomy truncated by the field of view, none of which have a boundary to refine.
The rest is already at the discretisation floor: the median per-label HD95 is one voxel and the third quartile two.
We measured this before instance optimisation, so it holds for the training objective as well.

\subsection{Comparison against Baselines}

We swept the loss weights and the instance-optimisation settings and selected the one reported below with the challenge ranking of Sec.~\ref{sec:model_selection}, applied to the surrogate protocol above.
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} places the three configurations against the baselines.
The container configuration improves DSC from $73.3\%$ (NiftyReg, the strongest baseline) to $81.0\%$ and reduces HD95 from $7.09$ to $6.17$\,mm, while also reducing the MTV and TLG errors and the non-diffeomorphic volume from $1525$ to $864$\,ppm.

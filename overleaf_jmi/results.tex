\section{Results}
\label{sect:results}

All results are on the test set (28 patients, 38 pairs); values are mean$\pm$SD over patients, and differences are given in percentage points.
Except where stated, IO uses predicted masks, while the reported alignment and PET-preservation metrics use the provided labels.
We first ask whether the losses alter the balance between alignment and scan-level PET change, and whether they should act during training or only during IO.
We then test whether that scan-level result extends to individual lesions, compare the selected method with baselines, and examine the effects of PET-loss weights and predicted-mask coverage.
The rigidity comparison separately tests how well the penalties preserve bone geometry.

\subsection{Effect of the PET-Preservation and Rigidity Losses}
\label{sect:res_q1}

Figure~\ref{fig:q2} compares four configurations (the full results with significance tests are in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}, Appendix~A): no PET-preservation or bone-rigidity loss, PET preservation alone, rigidity alone, and both.
Each network is evaluated before IO (squares; $\times$ without losses) and after IO (filled circles; $+$ without losses).
In IO, the PET-preservation and bone-rigidity losses are enabled or disabled to match that network's training configuration.
Without IO (squares vs.\ $\times$), training with the PET losses lowered the volume change of the lesions from 10.3\,\% to 8.2\,\% (significant) and the uptake change from 12.5\,\% to 9.8\,\% (not significant), at the cost of 1.9 Dice points.
IO raised the Dice of every network by 3.7 to 4.7 points (each square to the filled circle of the same color, $\times$ to $+$).
With the PET losses in training and IO (blue filled circle vs.\ $+$), $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ was 8.2\,\% instead of 11.3\,\% and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ 10.3\,\% instead of 14.5\,\%, for 2.5 Dice points; with IO, these reductions were not significant.
The rigidity loss alone (red) did not change the Dice of the network and cost 0.6 points with IO; it lowered $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ significantly for the network output (10.3\,\% to 8.9\,\%) and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ significantly with IO (14.5\,\% to 10.6\,\%).
Combining both groups (purple) preserved about as well as the PET losses alone and was the least accurate variant (73.6\,\% and 77.3\,\% Dice).
HD95 followed the Dice in every case (network 7.2 to 7.4~mm, with IO 6.6 to 7.1~mm).
All network outputs and the IO variants without the PET losses stayed below the challenge's NDV threshold of 0.005\,\% (50~ppm) on every pair; with the PET losses in IO, one pair each exceeded it (150 and 262~ppm), and the variant means were at most 10~ppm.

The losses therefore change the accuracy--preservation balance; the next question is whether they need to shape the trained network or can act only during per-pair refinement.

\subsection{Losses in Training or Only in IO}
\label{sect:res_q2}

To test whether the losses need to act during training, Fig.~\ref{fig:q2} compares, for each loss group, the network trained with the losses followed by IO with them (filled circles) with the network trained without them followed by the same IO (hollow circles); the second dashed segment of each color connects the two.
With both loss groups (purple), applying them only in IO increased Dice by 1.0 points (95\,\% CI 0.4 to 1.6) compared with using them in training and IO.
The paired differences (IO only minus training and IO) were $+0.8$ points in $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ (95\,\% CI $-0.4$ to 2.0) and $+1.0$ points in $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ ($-0.3$ to 2.6).
With PET losses alone (blue), the Dice gain was 0.9 points (0.1 to 1.7), while $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ increased by 1.1 ($-0.3$ to 2.6) and 1.5 ($-0.0$ to 3.4) points.
With rigidity alone (red), the paired differences were 0.0 ($-0.0$ to 0.1) Dice points, $+0.7$ ($-0.0$ to 1.8) MTV points, and $+0.8$ (0.0 to 1.9) TLG points.
None of the preservation differences was significant after Holm correction; the intervals do not establish equivalent preservation.
Without IO, training with the losses reduced registration-induced PET changes in the network-only variants tested (Fig.~\ref{fig:q2}, squares; Table~\ref{tab:ablations}).



\subsection{Per-Lesion Volume Change}
\label{sect:res_lesions}

To reveal changes hidden by scan-level aggregation, Fig.~\ref{fig:lesions} shows the volume change of 1327 lesions from 26 patients, after excluding 108 components smaller than five voxels.
Small lesions changed most in every condition: for bone-associated lesions with IO and without the losses ($+$), the median change was 30\,\% (small), 19\,\% (medium), and 10\,\% (large).
A small lesion has fewer than about 20 voxels, so gaining or losing one or two voxels is already a large relative change.
The per-lesion uptake change followed the volume change in all strata (Fig.~\ref{fig:lesions_tlg}, Appendix~B).
Without the losses, IO increased the distortion of lesions ($\times$ to $+$), most of all of small lesions (soft tissue from 17\,\% to 33\,\%, bone from 25\,\% to 30\,\%); bone lesions tended to grow (median signed change $+1$ to $+5$\,\%) and soft-tissue lesions to shrink ($-3$ to $-14$\,\%).
The rigidity loss (red) acted mainly on bone-associated lesions, as expected from its construction: with IO, it lowered the median change of small, medium, and large bone lesions from 30\,\% to 20\,\%, 19\,\% to 12\,\%, and 10\,\% to 5\,\%, and left medium and large soft-tissue lesions unchanged (19\,\% and 17\,\%).
The PET losses (blue) acted in both locations, already without IO (squares), and with IO, for example from 19\,\% to 10\,\% for medium soft-tissue lesions and from 30\,\% to 16\,\% for small bone lesions.
With both groups (purple), bone lesions changed by 14\,\%, 9\,\%, and 6\,\%, similar to the PET losses alone; only for large bone lesions, the rigidity loss alone changed them less (5\,\%).
With the losses, IO did not reduce the per-lesion change below that of the network output (circles vs.\ squares of the same color), in line with the unchanged scan-level change (Sec.~\ref{sect:res_q1}).
The PET losses in IO penalize the change in the total volume and uptake of the predicted lesion mask, so the growth of some lesions can offset the shrinkage of others.
Even with the ground-truth lesion masks in IO, which lowered the scan-level $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ to 0.1\,\% (Sec.~\ref{sect:res_tradeoff}), the median per-lesion change was 13.5\,\%, as with the predicted masks (13.7\,\%).

Adding per-lesion terms to IO did not change this either.
On the validation set, the selection chose ten times the base weight (median per-lesion $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 11.3\,\% vs.\ 12.5\,\% without the terms).
On the test set, the median per-lesion volume change fell only from 13.7\,\% to 13.3\,\%, it decreased for only 5 of 26 patients, and small lesions changed more (median 20.0\,\% vs.\ 16.7\,\%); the scan-level changes were unchanged ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 9.3\,\%, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ 12.0\,\% vs.\ 11.8\,\%), and Dice rose by 0.6 points.
At the base weight, the terms also had no effect with the ground-truth lesion masks (median 13.5\,\% with and without them).
The medians of small lesions take only a few distinct values (e.g.\ 12.5\,\%, 14.3\,\%, 33.3\,\%) because they are ratios of small voxel counts.

\subsection{Contribution of Each PET Term}
\label{sect:res_loo}
To identify which constraints account for preservation in IO, we remove one PET term at a time from IO on the network trained without the losses (Table~\ref{tab:loo}).
The mean-Jacobian term $\mathcal{L}_{\overline{J}}$ carried the preservation: without it, $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ increased from 9.3\,\% to 10.4\,\% and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ from 11.8\,\% to 13.5\,\% (both significant).
Without the voxel-wise term $\mathcal{L}_{J}$, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ increased to 12.3\,\% (significant), whereas $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ was unchanged (9.3\,\%).
The volume and uptake terms worked against preservation.
Without $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MTV}}$, Dice increased from 78.6\,\% to 79.3\,\% and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ decreased to 10.7\,\% (both significant; $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 8.6\,\%, not significant).
Without $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TLG}}$, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ decreased to 9.8\,\% (significant), while Dice (78.7\,\%) and $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ (8.6\,\%) did not change significantly.
Both terms measure the change on the linearly warped, soft mask and PET (Eq.~\ref{eq:mtv_tlg}), whereas the evaluation warps both by nearest neighbor; lowering the soft change therefore need not lower the evaluated one.

\subsection{Comparison with Baselines}
\label{sect:res_baselines}

To place our method in context, we compare it with CT-only registration baselines, with IO applied directly after affine pre-registration, and with our other variants (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}).
Our method, with the losses only in IO, reached 78.3\,\% CT Dice and 6.89~mm HD95, significantly better than NiftyReg (70.1\,\%, 8.85~mm), ConvexAdam (61.7\,\%, 9.16~mm), and the affine pre-registration (46.4\,\%, 14.00~mm); its $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ (8.7\,\%) and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ (11.3\,\%) did not differ significantly from those of NiftyReg (8.7\,\%, 10.2\,\%) and ConvexAdam (9.3\,\%, 10.8\,\%).
Compared with the same network before IO, our method gained 2.9 Dice points (95\,\% CI 2.3 to 3.4).
The paired changes in $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ were $-1.7$ points (95\,\% CI $-3.6$ to 0.3) and $-1.2$ points ($-3.6$ to 1.2), respectively, so an increase in scan-level PET change was not detected.
NiftyReg, tuned on the validation set, exceeded the folding threshold on 5 of 38 validation pairs and 8 of 38 test pairs (21\,\%), with up to 0.37\,\% NDV; ConvexAdam stayed below it on every pair.
Our method exceeded it on two test pairs (76 and 125~ppm), and the variant with the losses also in training on one (262~ppm).
IO directly after the affine pre-registration reached only 56.6\,\% Dice: within the same iteration budget, IO cannot replace the network.
Resampling alone already changes the measurements: the affine pre-registration changed MTV by 4.5\,\% and TLG by 4.4\,\%, the lowest of all methods.

Among our variants, IO without the losses was the most accurate (80.2\,\% Dice, significantly higher than our method) but changed the lesions most ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 11.3\,\%, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ 14.5\,\%).
The losses only in IO lowered these changes to 8.7\,\% and 11.3\,\% at a cost of 1.9 Dice points, and the losses also in training to 7.9\,\% and 10.3\,\% at a cost of 2.9 points; after correction for multiple comparisons, none of these differences in preservation was significant.
By the challenge's composite score, IO without the losses ranked first, our method second, and the variant with the losses in training and IO third, with overlapping bootstrap rank intervals (1--4, 1--4, and 1--5).
The ranking was decided by accuracy: apart from the affine pre-registration on $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$, no method won a significant comparison in preservation or folding.
The network alone needs 11~s per pair, as fast as ConvexAdam and ten times faster than NiftyReg (108~s); IO adds about 2.5~minutes.
Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} shows a test pair whose lesion volume grew tenfold between baseline and follow-up (from 2400 to 24\,191 voxels); it was chosen for this change, not for its registration result, and illustrates alignment.
The scans were initially far apart (CT Dice 3.5\,\%); our method aligned them to 83.7\,\% Dice (NiftyReg 70.8\,\%, ConvexAdam 65.7\,\%), and NDV remained below the challenge threshold.
The volume and uptake of the 163 follow-up lesions changed by $+2.6\,\%$ and $+6.6\,\%$, more than with IO without the losses ($-0.6\,\%$ and $+2.3\,\%$).
Before registration, the CT overlay (Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative}c) and the label residual (e) show misalignment across the whole trunk; after registration (d, f), the residual is reduced to thin bands at organ boundaries and to parts of the lumbar vertebrae, and the warped lesion contours lie in the corresponding bones (h).
The Jacobian determinant (g) shows fine-grained changes in the local volume scale of the sampling transform throughout the body, which IO adds to the smooth network field because it has no smoothness term ($\lambda_{\mathrm{smooth}}=0$).

\subsection{Rigidity Penalties}
\label{sect:res_rigidity}
We compare rigidity penalties by how well they preserve within-bone distances without sacrificing anatomical alignment or deforming tissue near bone boundaries.
In the finite-difference Staring penalty, 21.2\,\% of the stencil reads leave the bone of the center voxel (all 560 labeled challenge scans); eroding the bones removes this leakage but keeps only 35.5\,\% of the bone voxels and removes 23\,\% of the bones entirely (ribs: 2.4\,\% of the voxels kept, 58\,\% of the ribs removed).

On the validation set (Fig.~\ref{fig:rigidity}), the per-bone rigid fit reached a lower within-bone distortion than the Staring penalty at every Dice level, most clearly for long distances, where Staring did not get below 2.8\,\% while the rigid fit reached 1.8\,\%.
With erosion, the Staring penalty barely changed the distortion even at $\times100$ (short distances 8.7\,\% vs.\ 12.7\,\% without rigidity), and Dice stayed almost constant, because most of the skeleton is no longer constrained.
Within the two-point tolerance, the selection chose $\times3$ for Staring and the largest grid value, $\times100$, for the other two penalties.

On the test set, Fig.~\ref{fig:rigidity_measures}a shows each rigidity measure as a percentage of its value with IO without rigidity (dashed line at 100\,\%).
Each penalty was best on the measure it optimizes: the rigid fit lowered the rigid-fit residual to 53\,\%, Staring the Staring energy to 18\,\%.
On the distance distortion, which neither penalty optimizes, the rigid fit was best, with 41\,\% (short) and 42\,\% (long) of the distortion without rigidity, compared with 47\,\% and 73\,\% for Staring and 69\,\% and 80\,\% for Staring with erosion.
Fig.~\ref{fig:rigidity_measures}b shows the cost in alignment: the rigid fit and Staring lowered CT Dice by 1.6 and 2.0 points and bone Dice by about 4 points, whereas soft-tissue Dice was unchanged with Staring and rose by 0.9 points with the rigid fit; Staring with erosion cost almost no Dice but also constrained the bones least.

In absolute terms (Table~\ref{tab:rigidity_full}, Appendix~F), the rigid fit reduced the short-distance distortion from 13.4\,\% to 5.0\,\% and the long-distance distortion from 4.4\,\% to 1.8\,\%; all three penalties improved every rigidity measure significantly over IO without rigidity, and all pairwise differences in distortion between the penalties were significant.
Staring also removed folding inside bones (NDV in bone from 6.5 to 0.3~ppm), whereas the rigid fit, which constrains only the displacement, left it unchanged (9.6~ppm, not significant).

No penalty changed the lesion volume or uptake change significantly on the test set (Table~\ref{tab:rigidity_full}).
On the validation set (Fig.~\ref{fig:rigidity_preservation}), every penalty and weight changed the lesions less than IO without rigidity, but the change did not decrease with the weight; the drop from weight 0 to the smallest weight came mostly from two patients whose volume change fell by 27 and 10 points already with the rigid fit at $\times0.1$; without these two patients, all weights stayed within about one point of IO without rigidity ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 3.7 to 4.9\,\% vs.\ 4.4\,\%).
Per lesion, the penalties lowered the median volume change of bone-associated lesions only from 9.3\,\% to 7.7--8.8\,\%, whereas an exactly affine transformation (the affine pre-registration) changed them by a median of 1.0\,\%.

\subsection{Accuracy--Preservation Trade-off}
\label{sect:res_tradeoff}
We vary the PET-loss weights in IO to show how alignment and lesion preservation change together and whether stronger weights resolve the residual error.
Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff} varies the PET weights in IO on the network trained without the losses.
Already the smallest factor ($\times10^{-4}$) lowered the volume change of the predicted lesion masks from 9.3\,\% (IO without the PET losses) to 2.1\,\% and that of the ground-truth lesions from 11.3\,\% to 9.3\,\%, without a loss in Dice (80.2\,\%); $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ fell from 14.5\,\% to 12.9\,\%.
Larger factors reduced the change of the predicted masks further, to below 1\,\% from $\times0.01$ on, but not that of the ground-truth lesions (9.0 to 9.8\,\%), while Dice decreased steadily, to 78.6\,\% at $\times0.1$ and 78.0\,\% at $\times1$.
Compared with the network output before IO (dashed), IO with the PET losses thus canceled the increase that IO otherwise causes and ended slightly below it ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 9.3\,\% vs.\ 10.3\,\%, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ 11.8\,\% vs.\ 12.5\,\% at $\times0.1$), but not significantly (two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test, $p=0.17$ and $p=0.86$; lower for 14 and 13 of 26 patients), while gaining 3.2 Dice points.
The factor chosen on the validation set ($\times0.1$) thus cost 1.6 Dice points more than $\times10^{-4}$ for the same volume change (9.3\,\%) and 1.1 points less uptake change (11.8\,\%).
On the predicted masks, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ fell from 10.2\,\% to 5.2\,\% at $\times10^{-4}$ and to about 3\,\% from $\times0.1$ on.
The same gap between the labels IO optimizes and the ground truth appeared for the organs: without the PET losses, IO reached 92.9\,\% Dice on the predicted organ labels but 80.2\,\% on the ground-truth labels, starting from 79.0\,\% and 75.5\,\% for the network output.

In the diagnostic run with provided lesion masks (black diamonds), the measured changes fell from 9.3\,\% to 0.1\,\% for MTV and from 11.8\,\% to 4.5\,\% for TLG at the tuned factor; Dice rose from 78.6\,\% to 79.1\,\%.
At $\times1$, the changes were similar (0.2\,\% MTV, 4.0\,\% TLG; 78.6\,\% Dice), so a larger weight hardly reduced the remaining uptake change even with provided masks.
The near-zero volume change is that of the scan total, which this run optimizes directly; the change of individual lesions remained (Sec.~\ref{sect:res_lesions}).

\subsection{Lesion Coverage in Predicted Masks}
\label{sect:res_detection}

To locate the remaining registration-induced change, we group all 1435 provided lesions, including the 108 smaller than five voxels, by how much each overlaps the mask used by IO (Fig.~\ref{fig:detection}): 214 had no overlap (missed; 6\,\% of lesion volume), 155 had less than 50\,\% overlap (partly covered; 6\,\%), and 1066 had at least 50\,\% overlap (covered; 88\,\%).

PET IO reduced the change of covered lesions below that of the network output, from 9.1\,\% to 6.0\,\% for $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and from 11.0\,\% to 8.4\,\% for $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ (IO without and with the PET losses at the tuned weights; network output 7.9\,\% and 9.2\,\%).
Partly covered lesions changed as much with the PET losses as without them (12.5\,\% and 12.0\,\%), and missed lesions less (14.4\,\% and 17.0\,\%) but still more than at the network output (12.4\,\%).
The loss has no direct target for a missed lesion and only a partial target for a partly covered lesion.
After PET IO, missed lesions therefore contributed more than half of the remaining change: 5.2 of 9.3 points of $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and 6.2 of 11.8 points of $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$, although they make up only 6\,\% of the lesion volume.
Covered lesions contributed 3.3 and 5.4 points.
Scaling the PET weights from $\times0.1$ to $\times1$ changed none of these values by more than 0.3 points.

Without the PET losses, IO increased the change of every group over the network output, for covered lesions from 7.9\,\% to 9.1\,\% ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$) and from 9.2\,\% to 11.0\,\% ($|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$), and the overall change from 10.3\,\% to 11.3\,\% and from 12.5\,\% to 14.5\,\%.
This agrees with the per-lesion analysis (Sec.~\ref{sect:res_lesions}), in which IO without the losses increased the volume change of lesions.
In the diagnostic run with provided lesion masks, the contribution of lesions that nnU-Net missed fell from 5.2 to 1.2 points of $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and from 6.2 to 2.1 points of $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$, and that of covered lesions from 3.3 to 0.9 and from 5.4 to 3.8 points.
The near-zero overall change with provided masks does not mean that the lesions kept their volumes: if only the missed, partly covered, or covered lesions had changed, MTV would have changed by 1.2, 1.2, and 0.9 points, respectively. Some lesions therefore grew while others shrank, and their changes canceled in the scan total, which is what both the PET loss and the evaluation metric measure (see also Sec.~\ref{sect:res_lesions}).

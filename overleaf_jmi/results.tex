\section{Results}
\label{sect:results}

Unless stated otherwise, results are reported on the test set (28 patients, 38 pairs); values are mean$\pm$SD over patients, and differences are given in percentage points.
Validation data are used only for choices made before test evaluation and are identified explicitly.
Except where stated, IO uses predicted masks, while the reported alignment and PET-preservation metrics use the provided labels.
We first ask whether the losses alter the balance between alignment and image-level PET change, and whether they should act during training or only during IO.
We then compare the selected configuration with baselines and investigate why residual image-level and per-lesion changes remain.
Finally, we evaluate bone-rigidity penalties as an additional PET-preservation strategy and as a constraint on within-bone geometry.

\subsection{Effect of the PET-Preservation and Rigidity Losses}
\label{sect:res_q1}

We first test whether the PET-preservation and bone-rigidity losses reduce registration-induced changes in MTV and TLG and what they cost in alignment accuracy.
Figure~\ref{fig:q2} compares four configurations (the full results with significance tests are in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}, Appendix~A): no PET-preservation or bone-rigidity loss, PET preservation alone, rigidity alone, and both.
Each network is evaluated before IO (squares; $\times$ without losses) and after IO (filled circles; $+$ without losses).
In IO, the PET-preservation and bone-rigidity losses are enabled or disabled to match that network's training configuration.
Without IO (squares vs.\ $\times$), training with the PET losses lowered the volume change of the lesions from 10.3\,\% to 8.2\,\% (significant) and the uptake change from 12.5\,\% to 9.8\,\% (not significant), at the cost of 1.9 Dice points.
IO raised the Dice of every network by 3.7 to 4.7 points (each square to the filled circle of the same color, $\times$ to $+$).
With the PET losses in training and IO (blue filled circle vs.\ $+$), $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ was 8.2\,\% instead of 11.3\,\% and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ 10.3\,\% instead of 14.5\,\%, for 2.5 Dice points; with IO, these reductions were not significant.
The rigidity loss alone (red) did not change the Dice of the network and cost 0.6 points with IO; it lowered $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ significantly for the network output (10.3\,\% to 8.9\,\%) and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ significantly with IO (14.5\,\% to 10.6\,\%).
Combining both groups (purple) gave mean MTV and TLG changes close to those with the PET losses alone and was the least accurate variant (73.6\,\% and 77.3\,\% Dice).
HD95 followed the Dice in every case (network 7.2 to 7.4~mm, with IO 6.6 to 7.1~mm).
Among the matched configurations in this comparison, all network outputs and the IO variants without the PET losses stayed below the NDV threshold of 0.005\,\% (50~ppm) on every pair.
With the PET losses in IO, the PET-only and combined configurations each exceeded it on one pair (150 and 262~ppm), while their patient-mean NDV values were at most 10~ppm.

The losses therefore change the accuracy--preservation balance; the next question is whether they need to shape the trained network or can act only during per-pair refinement.

\subsection{Losses in Training or Only in IO}
\label{sect:res_q2}

To test whether the losses need to act during training, Fig.~\ref{fig:q2} compares two variants for each loss group.
Filled circles denote the network trained with the losses followed by IO with them, while hollow circles denote the network trained without them followed by the same IO.
The second dashed segment of each color connects these variants.
With both loss groups (purple), applying them only in IO increased Dice by 1.0 points (95\,\% CI 0.4 to 1.6) compared with using them in training and IO.
The paired differences (IO only minus training and IO) were $+0.8$ points in $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ (95\,\% CI $-0.4$ to 2.0) and $+1.0$ points in $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ ($-0.3$ to 2.6).
With PET losses alone (blue), the Dice gain was 0.9 points (0.1 to 1.7), while $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ increased by 1.1 ($-0.3$ to 2.6) and 1.5 ($-0.0$ to 3.4) points.
With rigidity alone (red), the paired differences were 0.0 ($-0.0$ to 0.1) Dice points, $+0.7$ ($-0.0$ to 1.8) MTV points, and $+0.8$ (0.0 to 1.9) TLG points.
None of the preservation differences was significant after Holm correction; the intervals do not establish equivalent preservation.
Without IO, training with the losses lowered the mean registration-induced PET changes in the network-only variants tested; detected reductions were limited to MTV with the PET losses, rigidity, or both (Fig.~\ref{fig:q2}, squares; Table~\ref{tab:ablations}).
The IO-only placement with both loss groups is therefore used as our selected method in the comparison with baselines.



\subsection{Comparison with Baselines}
\label{sect:res_baselines}

To place our method in context, we compare it with CT-only registration baselines, with IO applied directly after affine pre-registration, and with our other variants (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}).
Our method, with the losses only in IO, reached 78.3\,\% CT Dice and 6.89~mm HD95.
Both were significantly better than NiftyReg (71.8\,\%, 7.88~mm), ConvexAdam (60.3\,\%, 9.32~mm), and the affine pre-registration (46.4\,\%, 14.00~mm).
Its $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ (8.7\,\%) and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ (11.3\,\%) did not differ significantly from those of NiftyReg (10.7\,\%, 12.6\,\%) or ConvexAdam (8.5\,\%, 9.3\,\%).
Compared with the same network before IO, our method gained 2.9 Dice points (95\,\% CI 2.3 to 3.4).
The paired changes in $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ were $-1.7$ points (95\,\% CI $-3.6$ to 0.3) and $-1.2$ points ($-3.6$ to 1.2), respectively, so an increase in image-level PET change was not detected.
The NDV summaries in Table~\ref{tab:baselines} are means over patients, whereas threshold exceedances count individual registration pairs.
NiftyReg, tuned on the validation set, exceeded the folding threshold on 8 of 38 validation pairs and 13 of 38 test pairs (34\,\%), with up to 0.51\,\% NDV, and folded significantly more than our method; ConvexAdam stayed below the threshold on every pair.
Our method exceeded it on two test pairs (76 and 125~ppm), and the variant with the losses also in training on one (262~ppm).
IO directly after the affine pre-registration reached only 56.6\,\% Dice: within the same iteration budget, IO cannot replace the network.
Even the affine pre-registration changed MTV by 4.5\,\% and TLG by 4.4\,\%, the lowest values among the registered images.

Among our variants, IO without the losses was the most accurate (80.2\,\% Dice, significantly higher than our method) but changed the lesions most ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 11.3\,\%, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ 14.5\,\%).
The losses only in IO lowered these changes to 8.7\,\% and 11.3\,\% at a cost of 1.9 Dice points.
Using the losses in training and IO lowered them to 7.9\,\% and 10.3\,\% at a cost of 2.9 points.
After correction for multiple comparisons, none of these preservation differences was significant.
By the challenge's composite score, IO without the losses ranked first, our method second, and the variant with the losses in training and IO third, with overlapping bootstrap rank intervals (1--4, 1--4, and 1--5).
The ranking was decided by accuracy: apart from the affine pre-registration on $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$, no method won a significant comparison in preservation, and none in folding after the ranking's equivalence threshold.
The network alone needs 11~s per pair, as fast as ConvexAdam and more than ten times faster than NiftyReg (128~s); IO adds about 2.5~minutes.
Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} illustrates anatomical alignment in a test pair selected for its tenfold between-image change in lesion volume (from 2400 to 24\,191 voxels).
The images were initially far apart (CT Dice 3.5\,\%); our method aligned them to 83.7\,\% Dice (NiftyReg 72.3\,\%, ConvexAdam 64.5\,\%), and NDV remained below the challenge threshold.
Before registration, the CT image overlay (Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative}c) and label residual (e) show misalignment across the whole trunk.
After registration (d, f), the residual is reduced to thin bands at organ boundaries and parts of the lumbar vertebrae.
The warped lesion contours lie in the corresponding bones (h).
The Jacobian determinant (g) shows fine-grained changes in the local volume scale of the transformation throughout the body, which IO adds to the smooth network field because it has no smoothness term ($\lambda_{\mathrm{smooth}}=0$).

\subsection{Accuracy--Preservation Trade-off}
\label{sect:res_tradeoff}
In a post-hoc test-set sensitivity analysis, we vary the PET-loss weights in IO to show how alignment and lesion preservation change together and whether stronger weights resolve the residual error.
Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff} varies the PET weights in IO on the network trained without the losses without reselecting the method.
The smallest factor ($\times10^{-4}$) lowered the volume change of the predicted lesion masks from 9.3\,\% (IO without the PET losses) to 2.1\,\% and that of the provided lesions from 11.3\,\% to 9.3\,\%.
Mean Dice remained 80.2\,\%, while $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ fell from 14.5\,\% to 12.9\,\%.
Larger factors reduced the change of the predicted masks further, to below 1\,\% from $\times0.01$ on, but not that of the provided lesions (9.0 to 9.8\,\%), while Dice decreased steadily, to 78.6\,\% at $\times0.1$ and 78.0\,\% at $\times1$.
Compared with the network output before IO (dashed), IO with the PET losses gained 3.2 Dice points and had slightly lower mean changes ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 9.3\,\% vs.\ 10.3\,\%, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ 11.8\,\% vs.\ 12.5\,\% at $\times0.1$).
Neither PET difference was significant (two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test, uncorrected $p=0.17$ and $p=0.86$; lower for 14 and 13 of 26 patients).
The original validation grid contained no nonzero factor below $\times0.1$.
In this test-set sweep, the selected factor $\times0.1$ therefore cost 1.6 Dice points more than $\times10^{-4}$ for the same volume change (9.3\,\%) and 1.1 points less uptake change (11.8\,\%).
Because this comparison was made on the test set, it does not justify replacing the validation-selected factor.
On the predicted masks, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ fell from 10.2\,\% to 5.2\,\% at $\times10^{-4}$ and to about 3\,\% from $\times0.1$ on.
The same gap between the labels IO optimizes and the provided labels appeared for the organs: without the PET losses, IO reached 92.9\,\% Dice on the predicted organ labels but 80.2\,\% on the provided labels, starting from 79.0\,\% and 75.5\,\% for the network output.

In the diagnostic run with provided lesion masks (black diamonds), the measured changes fell from 9.3\,\% to 0.1\,\% for MTV and from 11.8\,\% to 4.5\,\% for TLG at the tuned factor; Dice rose from 78.6\,\% to 79.1\,\%.
At $\times1$, the changes were similar (0.2\,\% MTV, 4.0\,\% TLG; 78.6\,\% Dice), so a larger weight hardly reduced the remaining uptake change even with provided masks.
The near-zero volume change is that of the image total, which this run optimizes directly; the change of individual lesions remained (Sec.~\ref{sect:res_lesions}).
This gap between optimization on predicted masks and evaluation on provided labels motivates an analysis of lesion coverage.

\subsection{Lesion Coverage in Predicted Masks}
\label{sect:res_detection}

To locate the remaining registration-induced change, we group all 1435 provided lesions by their overlap with the mask used by IO (Fig.~\ref{fig:detection}).
This analysis includes the 108 lesions smaller than five voxels.
Of all lesions, 214 had no overlap (missed; 6\,\% of lesion volume), 155 had less than 50\,\% overlap (partly covered; 6\,\%), and 1066 had at least 50\,\% overlap (covered; 88\,\%).

PET IO reduced the change of covered lesions below that of the network output, from 9.1\,\% to 6.0\,\% for $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and from 11.0\,\% to 8.4\,\% for $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ (IO without and with the PET losses at the tuned weights; network output 7.9\,\% and 9.2\,\%).
Partly covered lesions changed as much with the PET losses as without them (12.5\,\% and 12.0\,\%), and missed lesions less (14.4\,\% and 17.0\,\%) but still more than at the network output (12.4\,\%).
The loss has no direct target for a missed lesion and only a partial target for a partly covered lesion.
After PET IO, missed lesions therefore contributed more than half of the remaining change: 5.2 of 9.3 points of $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and 6.2 of 11.8 points of $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$, although they make up only 6\,\% of the lesion volume.
Covered lesions contributed 3.3 and 5.4 points.
Scaling the PET weights from $\times0.1$ to $\times1$ changed none of these values by more than 0.3 points.

Without the PET losses, IO increased the change of every group over the network output, for covered lesions from 7.9\,\% to 9.1\,\% ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$) and from 9.2\,\% to 11.0\,\% ($|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$), and the overall change from 10.3\,\% to 11.3\,\% and from 12.5\,\% to 14.5\,\%.
This agrees with the per-lesion analysis (Sec.~\ref{sect:res_lesions}), in which lesions had higher volume changes after IO without the losses.
In the diagnostic run with provided lesion masks, the contribution of lesions that nnU-Net missed fell from 5.2 to 1.2 points of $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and from 6.2 to 2.1 points of $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$, and that of covered lesions from 3.3 to 0.9 and from 5.4 to 3.8 points.
The near-zero overall change with provided masks does not mean that individual lesions kept their volumes.
If only the missed, partly covered, or covered lesions had changed, MTV would have changed by 1.2, 1.2, and 0.9 points, respectively.
Some lesions therefore grew while others shrank, and their changes canceled in the image total measured by both the PET loss and the evaluation metric (see also Sec.~\ref{sect:res_lesions}).

As a post-hoc attempt to increase lesion coverage, we combined the nnU-Net predictions with voxels above SUV 3 outside a 10-mm halo around predicted organs with physiological uptake, following the recall-biased approach of Dey and Dalca~\cite{dey2026registration}.
On the moving test images with lesions (mean over patients), lesion detection increased from 74\,\% to 91\,\%, while voxel-level precision fell from 0.71 to 0.22.
Compared with IO using the nnU-Net mask, MTV change was 10.3\,\% instead of 9.3\,\%, TLG change was 12.5\,\% instead of 11.8\,\%, and CT Dice was 79.3\,\% instead of 78.6\,\%.
None of these differences was significant (two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test, uncorrected $p\geq0.09$).
The per-lesion median was similar (13.5\,\% vs.\ 13.7\,\%).
Thus, the tested recall-biased mask produced no detected reduction in either image-level or per-lesion change despite detecting more lesions.

\subsection{Per-Lesion Volume Change}
\label{sect:res_lesions}

Mask coverage explains part of the residual image-level change, but image totals can also hide opposing changes in different lesions.
To reveal these changes, Fig.~\ref{fig:lesions} shows the volume change of 1327 lesions from 26 patients, after excluding 108 components smaller than five voxels.
Small lesions changed most in every condition: for bone-associated lesions with IO and without the losses ($+$), the median change was 30\,\% (small), 19\,\% (medium), and 10\,\% (large).
A small lesion has fewer than about 20 voxels, so gaining or losing one or two voxels is already a large relative change.
The per-lesion uptake change followed the volume change in all strata (Fig.~\ref{fig:lesions_tlg}, Appendix~B).
Without the losses, IO increased lesion distortion ($\times$ to $+$), especially for small lesions (soft tissue from 17\,\% to 33\,\%, bone-associated from 25\,\% to 30\,\%).
Bone-associated lesions tended to grow (median signed change $+1$ to $+5$\,\%), whereas soft-tissue lesions tended to shrink ($-3$ to $-14$\,\%).
The rigidity loss (red) acted mainly on bone-associated lesions, as expected from its construction.
With IO, it lowered their median change for small, medium, and large lesions from 30\,\% to 20\,\%, 19\,\% to 12\,\%, and 10\,\% to 5\,\%.
It left medium and large soft-tissue lesions unchanged at 19\,\% and 17\,\%.
The PET losses (blue) acted in both locations, already without IO (squares), and with IO, for example from 19\,\% to 10\,\% for medium soft-tissue lesions and from 30\,\% to 16\,\% for small bone-associated lesions.
With both groups (purple), bone-associated lesions changed by 14\,\%, 9\,\%, and 6\,\%, similar to the PET losses alone; only for large bone-associated lesions, the rigidity loss alone changed them less (5\,\%).
With the losses, IO did not reduce the per-lesion change below that of the network output (circles vs.\ squares of the same color), while no image-level preservation difference was detected either (Sec.~\ref{sect:res_q1}).
The PET losses in IO penalize the change in the total volume and uptake of the predicted lesion mask, so the growth of some lesions can offset the shrinkage of others.
Even with the provided lesion masks in IO, which lowered the image-level $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ to 0.1\,\% (Sec.~\ref{sect:res_tradeoff}), the median per-lesion change was 13.5\,\%, as with the predicted masks (13.7\,\%).

We next tested whether terms on individual predicted components could prevent this cancellation in the intended test-time setting.
On the validation set, the selection chose ten times the base weight and reduced the median per-lesion $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ from 12.5\,\% to 11.3\,\%.
Adding the validation-selected per-lesion terms did not provide a consistent benefit on the test set.
The median per-lesion volume change fell only from 13.7\,\% to 13.3\,\% and decreased for only 5 of 26 patients, while the median for small lesions was higher (20.0\,\% vs.\ 16.7\,\%).
The mean image-level $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ remained at 9.3\,\%, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ changed from 11.8\,\% to 12.0\,\%, and Dice rose by 0.6 points.
In the separate diagnostic using provided lesion masks, the terms at their base weight also left the median per-lesion change unchanged at 13.5\,\%.
The medians of small lesions take only a few distinct values (e.g.\ 12.5\,\%, 14.3\,\%, 33.3\,\%) because they are ratios of small voxel counts.

\subsection{Contribution of Each PET Term}
\label{sect:res_loo}
To identify which constraints account for preservation in IO, we remove one PET term at a time from IO on the network trained without the losses (Table~\ref{tab:loo}).
The mean-Jacobian term $\mathcal{L}_{\overline{J}}$ carried the preservation: without it, $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ increased from 9.3\,\% to 10.4\,\% and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ from 11.8\,\% to 13.5\,\% (both significant).
Without the voxel-wise term $\mathcal{L}_{J}$, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ increased to 12.3\,\% (significant), whereas $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ was unchanged (9.3\,\%).
The two direct measurement terms did not improve the evaluated endpoints in this test-set ablation.
Without $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MTV}}$, Dice increased from 78.6\,\% to 79.3\,\% and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ decreased to 10.7\,\% (both significant; $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 8.6\,\%, not significant).
Without $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TLG}}$, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ decreased to 9.8\,\% (significant), while Dice (78.7\,\%) and $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ (8.6\,\%) did not change significantly.
Thus, the test-set results do not support a claim that every PET term improves the evaluated preservation metrics.
One possible explanation is that both direct terms measure change on the linearly warped, soft mask and PET image (Eq.~\ref{eq:mtv_tlg}), whereas the evaluation warps both by nearest neighbor.
The ablation is consistent with this surrogate mismatch but does not establish it as the cause.
Because the four-term configuration was fixed before test evaluation, this diagnostic ablation was not used to select a reduced loss.

\subsection{Rigidity Penalties}
\label{sect:res_rigidity}
Bones should retain their internal geometry during registration even when they move relative to one another.
Because label overlap does not measure this property, we compare rigidity penalties by how well they preserve within-bone distances and report the accompanying changes in bone and soft-tissue alignment.
In the finite-difference Staring penalty, 21.2\,\% of the stencil reads leave the bone of the center voxel across all 560 labeled challenge images.
Eroding the bones removes this leakage but retains only 35.5\,\% of the bone voxels and removes 23\,\% of the bones entirely.
For ribs, it retains 2.4\,\% of the voxels and removes 58\,\% of the structures.

On the validation set (Fig.~\ref{fig:rigidity}), the per-bone rigid fit reached a lower within-bone distortion than the Staring penalty at every Dice level, most clearly for long distances, where Staring did not get below 2.8\,\% while the rigid fit reached 1.8\,\%.
With erosion, the Staring penalty barely changed the distortion even at $\times100$ (short distances 8.7\,\% vs.\ 12.7\,\% without rigidity), and Dice stayed almost constant, because most of the skeleton is no longer constrained.
Within the two-point tolerance, the selection chose $\times3$ for Staring and the largest grid value, $\times100$, for the other two penalties.

On the test set, Fig.~\ref{fig:rigidity_measures}a shows each rigidity measure as a percentage of its value with IO without rigidity (dashed line at 100\,\%).
Each penalty produced the lowest value for the measure it optimizes: the rigid fit lowered the rigid-fit residual to 53\,\%, and Staring lowered the Staring energy to 18\,\%.
Neither penalty directly optimizes distance distortion.
The rigid fit nevertheless produced the lowest values: 41\,\% (short) and 42\,\% (long) of the distortion without rigidity, compared with 47\,\% and 73\,\% for Staring and 69\,\% and 80\,\% for Staring with erosion.
Fig.~\ref{fig:rigidity_measures}b shows the cost in alignment.
The rigid fit and Staring lowered CT Dice by 1.6 and 2.0 points and bone Dice by about 4 points.
Soft-tissue Dice was unchanged with Staring and rose by 0.9 points with the rigid fit.
Staring with erosion cost almost no Dice but also constrained the bones least.

In absolute terms (Table~\ref{tab:rigidity_full}, Appendix~F), the rigid fit reduced the short-distance distortion from 13.4\,\% to 5.0\,\% and the long-distance distortion from 4.4\,\% to 1.8\,\%.
All three penalties improved every rigidity measure significantly over IO without rigidity.
All pairwise distortion differences between the penalties were also significant.
Staring also removed folding inside bones (NDV in bone from 6.5 to 0.3~ppm), whereas the rigid fit, which constrains only the displacement, left it unchanged (9.6~ppm, not significant).

When added on top of the PET losses, none of the three rigidity penalties produced a detected additional change in lesion volume or uptake preservation on the test set (Table~\ref{tab:rigidity_full}).
This comparison differs from Fig.~\ref{fig:q2}, where rigidity used without the PET losses significantly reduced network-output $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and IO $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ relative to the corresponding unconstrained variants.
On the validation set (Fig.~\ref{fig:rigidity_preservation}), every penalty and weight changed the lesions less than IO without rigidity, but the change did not decrease with increasing weight.
The drop between weight 0 and the smallest weight came mostly from two patients whose volume change fell by 27 and 10 points with the rigid fit at $\times0.1$.
Without these patients, all weights remained within about one point of IO without rigidity ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 3.7 to 4.9\,\% vs.\ 4.4\,\%).
Per lesion, the penalties lowered the median volume change of bone-associated lesions only from 9.3\,\% to 7.7--8.8\,\%, whereas an exactly affine transformation (the affine pre-registration) changed them by a median of 1.0\,\%.

\section{Results}
\label{sect:results}

All results are on the test set (28 patients, 38 pairs); values are mean$\pm$SD over patients, and differences are given in percentage points.

\subsection{Effect of the PET-Preservation and Rigidity Losses}
\label{sect:res_q1}

Table~\ref{tab:ablations} shows the four networks trained with neither, the PET-preservation, the rigidity, or both groups of losses, as network output and with IO using the same losses.
Training with the PET losses lowered the volume change of the lesions from 10.6\,\% to 7.6\,\% and the uptake change from 10.8\,\% to 7.6\,\%, at the cost of 1.9 Dice points.
IO raised the Dice of every network by 2.8 to 4.4 points and kept the effect of the losses: with the PET losses, $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ was 6.3\,\% instead of 10.2\,\% and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ 7.2\,\% instead of 11.9\,\%, for 2.8 Dice points.
The rigidity loss alone did not change the Dice of the network and cost 0.5 points with IO, and it lowered $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ in both cases (network 9.4\,\%, with IO 7.9\,\%).
Combining both groups preserved about as well as the PET losses alone and was the least accurate variant (72.8\,\% and 75.6\,\% Dice).
HD95 followed the Dice in every case (network 7.2 to 7.5~mm, with IO 6.8 to 7.2~mm).
No variant folded: the non-diffeomorphic volume of every pair stayed below the 0.005\,\% threshold of the challenge (at most 0.6~ppm on average).

Selecting the network without the losses at its checkpoint with the best validation tumour preservation, instead of the best accuracy, is no substitute for the losses: it cost 3.0 Dice points while $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ fell by only 0.4 and 0.9 points (not significant).

\subsection{Losses in Training or Only in IO}
\label{sect:res_q2}

Figure~\ref{fig:q2} compares, for each group of losses, the network trained with the losses (followed by IO with them) with the network trained without them, followed by the same IO.
Using the losses only in IO was more accurate: Dice increased by 1.2 points [95\,\% CI 0.4, 1.9] for the PET losses and by 1.7 points [1.1, 2.2] for both groups (both significant), and was unchanged for rigidity.
Preservation was not significantly worse: $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ increased by 1.3 [0.0, 2.6], 0.5 [0.0, 1.0], and 0.8 [$-1.0$, 2.7] points for PET, rigidity, and both, and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ by at most 1.5 points.
Without IO, in contrast, the training losses are the only means of preservation (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}, left).

\subsection{Per-Lesion Volume Change}
\label{sect:res_lesions}

Figure~\ref{fig:lesions} shows the volume change of the 1327 individual lesions (26 patients with lesions).
Small lesions changed most in every condition: for bone-associated lesions with IO and without the losses, the median change was 33\,\% (small), 17\,\% (medium), and 9\,\% (large).
A small lesion has fewer than about 20 voxels, so gaining or losing one or two voxels is already a large relative change.
Without the losses, IO increased the distortion of lesions, most of all of small bone lesions (median from 20\,\% to 33\,\%); bone lesions tended to grow (median signed change $+3$ to $+10$\,\%) and soft-tissue lesions to shrink ($-9$ to $-13$\,\%).
The rigidity loss acted only on bone-associated lesions, as expected from its construction: with IO, it lowered the median change of small, medium, and large bone lesions from 33\,\% to 20\,\%, 17\,\% to 12\,\%, and 9\,\% to 5\,\%, and left soft-tissue lesions unchanged (17 to 22\,\%).
The PET losses acted in both locations, for example from 18\,\% to 10\,\% for medium soft-tissue lesions and from 33\,\% to 13\,\% for small bone lesions; only for small soft-tissue lesions, the smallest group (41 lesions), no clear effect was visible.
With both groups, bone lesions changed least at every size (10\,\%, 9\,\%, and 4\,\%), and their systematic growth disappeared (median signed change about 0).
The medians of small lesions take only a few distinct values (e.g.\ 10.0\,\%, 12.5\,\%, 20.0\,\%) because they are ratios of small voxel counts.

\subsection{Comparison with Baselines}
\label{sect:res_baselines}

Table~\ref{tab:baselines} and Fig.~\ref{fig:baselines_distribution} compare our variants with the baselines.
Every variant of ours was significantly more accurate than NiftyReg (69.4\,\% Dice, 8.6~mm HD95), ConvexAdam (61.7\,\%, 9.2~mm), and the affine pre-registration (46.4\,\%, 13.9~mm), and none of them folded.
NiftyReg, tuned on the validation set where it rarely folded, exceeded the folding threshold on 21\,\% of the test pairs (mean 1170~ppm, driven by three pairs with 0.8 to 1.3\,\%).
IO directly after the affine pre-registration reached only 52.7\,\% Dice: within the same iteration budget, IO cannot replace the network.
Its MTV and TLG change was the lowest of all methods (3.4\,\%), because the deformation it adds to the affine is small.

Among our variants, IO without the PET and rigidity losses was the most accurate (79.2\,\% Dice), and our full method, with the losses in training and IO, preserved best ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 6.2\,\%, significantly lower than without the losses).
The variant with the losses only in IO ranked first (77.3\,\% Dice, 7.0\,\% $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$), followed by the full method and the variant without the losses; their rank intervals overlap (1--5).
The ranking was decided mainly by accuracy: after correction for multiple comparisons, few differences in preservation were significant (on $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$, each variant with the losses outperformed one other method and IO after the affine two; on $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$, only IO after the affine outperformed others), and none in folding.
The network alone needs 11~s per pair, as fast as ConvexAdam and ten times faster than NiftyReg (108~s); IO adds about 2.5~minutes.

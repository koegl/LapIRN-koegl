\section{Results}
\label{sect:results}

All results are on the test set (28 patients, 38 pairs); values are mean$\pm$SD over patients, and differences are given in percentage points.
Except where stated, IO uses predicted masks, while the reported alignment and PET-preservation metrics use the provided labels.
We first test whether the losses reduce changes to lesion measurements and where they should act in the registration pipeline.
We then examine individual lesions and PET loss terms, compare with baseline methods, assess alternative rigidity penalties and the accuracy--preservation trade-off, and relate the remaining error to predicted lesion-mask coverage.

\subsection{Effect of the PET-Preservation and Rigidity Losses}
\label{sect:res_q1}

Figure~\ref{fig:q2} compares four configurations (the full results with significance tests are in Table~\ref{tab:ablations}, Appendix~A): no PET-preservation or bone-rigidity loss, PET preservation alone, rigidity alone, and both.
Each network is evaluated before IO (squares; $\times$ without losses) and after IO (filled circles; $+$ without losses).
In IO, the PET-preservation and bone-rigidity losses are enabled or disabled to match that network's training configuration.
Without IO (squares vs.\ $\times$), training with the PET losses lowered the volume change of the lesions from 10.3\,\% to 8.2\,\% (significant) and the uptake change from 12.5\,\% to 9.8\,\% (not significant), at the cost of 1.9 Dice points.
IO raised the Dice of every network by 3.7 to 4.7 points (each square to the filled circle of the same colour, $\times$ to $+$).
With the PET losses in training and IO (blue filled circle vs.\ $+$), $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ was 8.2\,\% instead of 11.3\,\% and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ 10.3\,\% instead of 14.5\,\%, for 2.5 Dice points; with IO, these reductions were not significant.
The rigidity loss alone (red) did not change the Dice of the network and cost 0.6 points with IO; it lowered $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ significantly for the network output (10.3\,\% to 8.9\,\%) and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ significantly with IO (14.5\,\% to 10.6\,\%).
Combining both groups (purple) preserved about as well as the PET losses alone and was the least accurate variant (73.6\,\% and 77.3\,\% Dice).
HD95 followed the Dice in every case (network 7.2 to 7.4~mm, with IO 6.6 to 7.1~mm).
All network outputs and the IO variants without the PET losses stayed below the challenge's NDV threshold of 0.005\,\% (50~ppm) on every pair; with the PET losses in IO, one pair each exceeded it (150 and 262~ppm), and the variant means were at most 10~ppm.

The losses therefore change the accuracy--preservation balance; the next question is whether they need to shape the trained network or can act only during per-pair refinement.

\subsection{Losses in Training or Only in IO}
\label{sect:res_q2}

To test whether the losses need to act during training, Fig.~\ref{fig:q2} compares, for each loss group, the network trained with the losses followed by IO with them (filled circles) with the network trained without them followed by the same IO (hollow circles); the second dashed segment of each colour connects the two.
With both loss groups (purple), applying them only in IO increased Dice by 1.0 points (95\,\% CI 0.4 to 1.6) compared with using them in training and IO.
The paired differences (IO only minus training and IO) were $+0.8$ points in $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ (95\,\% CI $-0.4$ to 2.0) and $+1.0$ points in $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ ($-0.3$ to 2.6).
With PET losses alone (blue), the Dice gain was 0.9 points (0.1 to 1.7), while $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ increased by 1.1 ($-0.3$ to 2.6) and 1.5 ($-0.0$ to 3.4) points.
With rigidity alone (red), the paired differences were 0.0 ($-0.0$ to 0.1) Dice points, $+0.7$ ($-0.0$ to 1.8) MTV points, and $+0.8$ (0.0 to 1.9) TLG points.
None of the preservation differences was significant after Holm correction; the intervals do not establish equivalent preservation.
Without IO, training with the losses reduced registration-induced PET changes in the network-only variants tested (Fig.~\ref{fig:q2}, squares; Table~\ref{tab:ablations}).



\subsection{Per-Lesion Volume Change}
\label{sect:res_lesions}

To reveal changes hidden by scan-level aggregation, Fig.~\ref{fig:lesions} shows the volume change of 1327 lesions from 26 patients, after excluding 108 components smaller than five voxels.
Small lesions changed most in every condition: for bone-associated lesions with IO and without the losses ($+$), the median change was 30\,\% (small), 19\,\% (medium), and 10\,\% (large).
A small lesion has fewer than about 20 voxels, so gaining or losing one or two voxels is already a large relative change.
Without the losses, IO increased the distortion of lesions ($\times$ to $+$), most of all of small lesions (soft tissue from 17\,\% to 33\,\%, bone from 25\,\% to 30\,\%); bone lesions tended to grow (median signed change $+1$ to $+5$\,\%) and soft-tissue lesions to shrink ($-3$ to $-14$\,\%).
The rigidity loss (red) acted mainly on bone-associated lesions, as expected from its construction: with IO, it lowered the median change of small, medium, and large bone lesions from 30\,\% to 20\,\%, 19\,\% to 12\,\%, and 10\,\% to 5\,\%, and left medium and large soft-tissue lesions unchanged (19\,\% and 17\,\%).
The PET losses (blue) acted in both locations, already without IO (squares), and with IO, for example from 19\,\% to 10\,\% for medium soft-tissue lesions and from 30\,\% to 16\,\% for small bone lesions.
With both groups (purple), bone lesions changed by 14\,\%, 9\,\%, and 6\,\%, similar to the PET losses alone; only for large bone lesions, the rigidity loss alone changed them less (5\,\%).
With the losses, IO did not reduce the per-lesion change below that of the network output (circles vs.\ squares of the same colour), in line with the unchanged scan-level change (Sec.~\ref{sect:res_q1}).
The PET losses in IO penalise the change in the total volume and uptake of the predicted lesion mask, so the growth of some lesions can offset the shrinkage of others.
Even with the ground-truth lesion masks in IO, which lowered the scan-level $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ to 0.1\,\% (Sec.~\ref{sect:res_tradeoff}), the median per-lesion change was 13.5\,\%, as with the predicted masks (13.7\,\%).
The medians of small lesions take only a few distinct values (e.g.\ 12.5\,\%, 14.3\,\%, 33.3\,\%) because they are ratios of small voxel counts.

\subsection{Contribution of Each PET Term}
\label{sect:res_loo}
To identify which constraints account for preservation in IO, we remove one PET term at a time from IO on the network trained without the losses (Table~\ref{tab:loo}).
The two Jacobian terms carried the volume preservation: without $\mathcal{L}_{\overline{J}}$, $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ increased from 7.6\,\% to 8.3\,\%, and without $\mathcal{L}_{J}$ to 7.8\,\% (both significant).
The volume term $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MTV}}$ mainly cost accuracy: without it, Dice increased by 1.3 points (from 77.5\,\% to 78.8\,\%, significant), while $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ increased only from 7.6\,\% to 8.0\,\% and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ decreased from 8.7\,\% to 8.2\,\% (neither significant).
Switching off $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TLG}}$ changed no metric significantly ($|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ 9.1\,\%, $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 7.2\,\%).
No single term changed $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ significantly, and all changes of $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ were below one percentage point.

\subsection{Comparison with Baselines}
\label{sect:res_baselines}

To place the alignment--preservation trade-off among our variants in context, we compare them with CT-only registration baselines and with IO applied directly after affine pre-registration (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}, Fig.~\ref{fig:baselines_distribution}).
Every variant of ours had higher mean CT Dice and lower mean HD95 than NiftyReg (69.4\,\% Dice, 8.6~mm HD95), ConvexAdam (61.7\,\%, 9.2~mm), and the affine pre-registration (46.4\,\%, 13.9~mm), and stayed below the folding threshold on every test pair.
NiftyReg, tuned on the validation set, exceeded the threshold on 5 of 38 validation pairs and 8 of 38 test pairs (21\,\%); its test-set NDV averaged 1170~ppm over patients, driven by three pairs with 0.8 to 1.3\,\% NDV.
IO directly after the affine pre-registration reached only 52.7\,\% Dice: within the same iteration budget, IO cannot replace the network.
Its MTV and TLG change was the lowest of all methods (3.4\,\%), because the deformation it adds to the affine is small.

Among our variants, IO without the PET and rigidity losses was the most accurate (79.2\,\% Dice), and our full method, with the losses in training and IO, preserved best ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 6.2\,\%, significantly lower than without the losses).
By the composite score, the variant with the losses only in IO ranked first (77.3\,\% Dice, 7.0\,\% $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$), while the full method and basic IO tied for second; all three had bootstrap rank intervals of 1--5, so this rank order is descriptive.
The ranking was decided mainly by accuracy: after correction for multiple comparisons, few differences in preservation were significant (on $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$, each variant with the losses outperformed one other method and IO after the affine two; on $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$, only IO after the affine outperformed others), and none in folding.
The network alone needs 11~s per pair, as fast as ConvexAdam and ten times faster than NiftyReg (108~s); IO adds about 2.5~minutes.
Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} shows a test pair whose lesion volume grew tenfold between baseline and follow-up (from 2400 to 24\,191 voxels).
The scans were initially far apart (CT Dice 3.5\,\%); our full method aligned them to 81.0\,\% Dice (NiftyReg 69.5\,\%, ConvexAdam 65.6\,\%), while the volume and uptake of the 163 follow-up lesions changed by only $-0.1\,\%$ and $+0.9\,\%$; NDV remained below the challenge threshold.
Before registration, the CT overlay (Fig.~\ref{fig:qualitative}c) and the label residual (e) show misalignment across the whole trunk; after registration (d, f), the residual is reduced to thin bands at organ boundaries.
The remaining residual lies mostly at organ boundaries and in the lumbar vertebrae (f), and the lesions keep their shape after warping (h).
The Jacobian determinant (g) shows fine-grained changes in the local volume scale of the sampling transform throughout the body, which IO adds to the smooth network field because it has no smoothness term ($\lambda_{\mathrm{smooth}}=0$).

\subsection{Accuracy--Preservation Trade-off}
\label{sect:res_tradeoff}
We vary the PET-loss weights in IO to show how alignment and lesion preservation change together and whether stronger weights resolve the residual error.
Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff} varies the PET weights in IO on the network trained without the losses.
Already the smallest factor ($\times10^{-4}$) lowered the volume change of the predicted lesion masks from 8.6\,\% (IO without the PET losses) to 2.2\,\% and that of the ground-truth lesions from 10.2\,\% to 8.0\,\%, at a cost of 0.1 Dice points; $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ fell from 11.8\,\% to 8.9\,\%.
Larger factors reduced the change of the predicted masks further, to 1.0\,\% from $\times0.1$ on, but hardly that of the ground-truth lesions (7.6\,\% at $\times0.1$, 7.5\,\% at $\times1$), while Dice decreased steadily, to 77.5\,\% at $\times0.1$.
The factor chosen on the validation set ($\times0.1$) thus cost 1.6 Dice points more than $\times10^{-4}$ for 0.4 points less volume change.
On the predicted masks, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ stayed at about 3\,\% for all factors, close to the resampling floor of 2.9\,\%.
The same gap between the labels IO optimises and the ground truth appeared for the organs: without the PET losses, IO reached 90.5\,\% Dice on the predicted organ labels but 79.2\,\% on the ground-truth labels, starting from 79.0\,\% and 74.8\,\% for the network output.

In the diagnostic run with provided lesion masks (black diamonds), the measured changes fell from 7.6\,\% to 2.1\,\% for MTV and from 8.7\,\% to 3.2\,\% for TLG at the tuned factor; Dice rose from 77.5\,\% to 78.1\,\%.
At $\times1$, preservation was unchanged (2.1\,\% MTV, 3.2\,\% TLG; 77.7\,\% Dice), so a larger weight did not remove the residual change even with provided masks.

\subsection{Lesion Coverage in Predicted Masks}
\label{sect:res_detection}

To locate the remaining registration-induced change, we group all 1435 provided lesions, including the 108 smaller than five voxels, by how much each overlaps the mask used by IO (Fig.~\ref{fig:detection}): 214 had no overlap (missed; 6\,\% of lesion volume), 155 had less than 50\,\% overlap (partly covered; 6\,\%), and 1066 had at least 50\,\% overlap (covered; 88\,\%).

PET IO reduced the change only of covered lesions, from 7.8\,\% to 4.9\,\% for $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and from 7.9\,\% to 5.1\,\% for $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ (IO without and with the PET losses at the tuned weights).
Missed and partly covered lesions changed as much with the PET losses as without them (missed: 14.9\,\% and 15.7\,\%; partly covered: 11.3\,\% and 11.5\,\%).
The loss has no direct target for a missed lesion and only a partial target for a partly covered lesion.
After PET IO, missed lesions therefore contributed most of the remaining change: 4.8 of 7.6 points of $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and 5.7 of 8.7 points of $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$, although they make up only 6\,\% of the lesion volume.
Covered lesions contributed 2.6 and 3.2 points.
Scaling the PET weights from $\times0.1$ to $\times1$ changed none of these values by more than 0.2 points.

Without the PET losses, IO increased the change of covered lesions over the network output, from 7.4\,\% to 7.8\,\% ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$) and from 6.9\,\% to 7.9\,\% ($|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$), and the overall $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ from 10.8\,\% to 11.9\,\%; the overall $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ fell slightly, from 10.6\,\% to 10.2\,\%, because missed and partly covered lesions changed less.
This agrees with the per-lesion analysis (Sec.~\ref{sect:res_lesions}), in which IO without the losses increased the volume change of lesions.
In the diagnostic run with provided lesion masks, the contribution of lesions that nnU-Net missed fell from 4.8 to 1.5 points of $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$, and that of covered lesions from 2.6 to 1.2 points.
Mask errors therefore account for much of the measured change in both groups, although neither contribution disappeared.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sect:intro}

Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) PET/CT is now routinely used for staging prostate cancer, for planning and following radioligand therapy, and for assessing response~\cite{ref_psma_clinical}.
Patients therefore accumulate a baseline examination and one or more follow-up examinations, and registering each follow-up to its baseline makes it possible to compare tumour burden voxel by voxel, to track individual lesions, and to accumulate dose~\cite{ref_psmareg_challenge}.
For these uses, a registration must do two things at once: it has to align the anatomy, and it must not itself change the PET-derived quantities that are compared across time points.
Two such quantities are the metabolic tumour volume (MTV) and the total lesion glycolysis (TLG).
A deformation that compresses or stretches a lesion changes both, so a change measured after registration can be an artefact of the registration rather than of the disease.
The Learn2Reg 2026 PSMAReg challenge~\cite{ref_learn2reg_hansen,ref_learn2reg_alessa,ref_psmaregdata} formalises this setting: each follow-up scan is warped to its baseline by a dense displacement field, which is scored on organ overlap, boundary distance, folding, and the change of MTV and TLG caused by the warp.

Classical registration solves an optimisation problem per image pair~\cite{ref_demons,ref_syn,bspline}; this is accurate but slow for whole-body scans.
Learning-based registration replaces the per-pair optimisation with one forward pass of a trained network~\cite{ref_voxelmorph_cvpr,ref_svf_voxelmorph,ref_transmorph}, and instance optimisation (IO) can refine the network output per pair at test time.
The coarse-to-fine Laplacian pyramid network LapIRN~\cite{ref_lapirn} recovers the large deformations between whole-body scans while keeping the transformation smooth and invertible.

In a challenge submission~\cite{ref_koegl_arxiv}, we adapted LapIRN to PSMA PET/CT and added losses that preserve the PET biomarkers of lesions and keep bones rigid.
That submission was developed and evaluated within the challenge protocol.
Here, we study these losses in a controlled setting: a new patient-level split with a held-out test set, retrained networks, tuned baselines, and statistics at the patient level.
We ask two questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Do the PET-preservation and bone-rigidity losses improve the complete registration pipeline, and what do they cost in alignment accuracy?
  \item Do the losses have to be part of network training, or is it sufficient to use them in instance optimisation?
\end{enumerate}

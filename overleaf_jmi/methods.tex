\section{Methods}
\label{sect:methods}

\subsection{Data and Split}
\label{sect:data}

We used the training set of the PSMAReg challenge~\cite{ref_psmareg_challenge,ref_psmaregdata}, a longitudinal whole-body PSMA PET/CT cohort.
Each scan consists of a CT and a PET image (in SUV), resampled to $2.7344\times2.7344\times3.27$~mm and cropped to $192\times192\times288$ voxels.
For every scan, the challenge provides 117 CT organ labels (the TotalSegmentator~\cite{ref_totalsegmentator} classes) and a PSMA lesion mask.
Following the challenge, a follow-up scan is always the moving image and the corresponding baseline scan the fixed image.
The original dataset publication reports that release of the anonymized retrospective imaging data was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Medical Faculty, LMU Munich (applications 21-1230 and 21-0098), and by the institutional data security and privacy review board~\cite{ref_psma_dataset_article}.
It also reports that the ethics committee waived individual informed consent because the images were fully anonymized.
The present study is a secondary analysis of publicly released challenge data and involved no patient recruitment or contact.

We split the 146 labeled patients with at least two scans at the patient level into training, validation, and test sets, aiming at 70/15/15\,\% of the registration pairs and balancing the sets for tumor burden and for bone- versus soft-tissue lesion location.
Training uses all pairs of later onto earlier examinations of a patient; validation and testing use only follow-up-to-baseline pairs, as in the challenge.
This gave 92/26/28 patients and 178/38/38 pairs.
The split was built once with a fixed seed and frozen before any experiment of this study; all choices of settings, checkpoints, and methods were made on the validation set, and the test set was used only for the final evaluation.
All patients were part of the challenge training data used to develop the earlier version of the method~\cite{ref_koegl_arxiv}; the test patients were not used for any decision in the present study.

\paragraph{Labels used by the method and for evaluation.}
Network training uses the provided organ and lesion labels of the training patients.
Instance optimization (Sec.~\ref{sect:io}) also needs organ and lesion labels of the scans being registered; in the main experiments, IO uses only predicted labels, while the provided labels of validation and test scans are used for evaluation (Sec.~\ref{sect:metrics}).
Organ labels are predicted from the CT by TotalSegmentator~\cite{ref_totalsegmentator}.
Lesion masks are predicted from PET/CT by an nnU-Net~\cite{nnunet} set up as the winning autoPET~III solution~\cite{rokuss2024fdgpsmahitchhikersguide}: a residual-encoder U-Net (ResEnc-L) with $192^3$ patches, initialized from their pretrained weights, with an auxiliary organ-segmentation head and misalignment augmentation.
It was trained with patient-grouped five-fold cross-validation on all 494 scans of the non-test patients, including patients with a single examination.
Validation scans are segmented by the fold model that did not see the patient, and test scans by the ensemble of the five fold models.
For validation, this prevents registration and IO tuning from relying on lesion predictions made by a model trained on the same patient.
The predicted lesion masks reach a mean Dice of 0.56 on the validation scans and 0.51 on the test scans (medians 0.69 and 0.67).

\paragraph{Preprocessing.}
For the network and IO, CT was clipped to $[-1000,1500]$~HU and PET to $[0,20]$~SUV, and both were rescaled to $[0,1]$.
As in the ConvexAdam baseline of the challenge~\cite{ref_convexadam}, the patient bed was removed from the CT.
All PET quantities in the evaluation are computed on the original SUV images.

\paragraph{PET measures.}
The PSMAReg challenge uses metabolic tumor volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG) to measure how registration changes the moving scan's lesion volume and uptake~\cite{ref_psmareg_challenge}.
For a PET image $P$ in SUV, binary lesion mask $M$, and voxel volume $v$, they are
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{MTV}(M) = v\sum_x M(x), \qquad
  \mathrm{TLG}(P,M) = v\sum_x P(x)M(x).
  \label{eq:pet_measures}
\end{equation}
Both quantities are computed on the same moving scan before and after warping it to the fixed frame; as in the challenge scorer, both PET and the lesion mask are warped by nearest-neighbor interpolation.
The reported preservation error for $Q\in\{\mathrm{MTV},\mathrm{TLG}\}$ is its absolute relative change,
\begin{equation}
  |\Delta Q| = \frac{|Q_{\mathrm{after}} - Q_{\mathrm{before}}|}{\max(Q_{\mathrm{before}},10^{-6})}.
  \label{eq:pet_error}
\end{equation}
This measures change induced by registration, not change in disease burden between examinations.

\subsection{Registration Pipeline}
\label{sect:pipeline}

The pipeline (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}) has three stages, all estimated from the moving scan $(C_m, P_m)$ and the fixed scan $(C_f, P_f)$, where $C$ denotes CT and $P$ PET.
We denote the affine sampling transform by $\varphi_{\mathrm{aff}}$, the network sampling transform by $\varphi_\theta$, and their composition by $\varphi$.
For each location $x$ on the fixed grid, $\varphi(x)$ gives the location sampled in the original moving scan: the warped image is $\widetilde C_m(x)=C_m(\varphi(x))$, and the same convention applies to PET and labels.

\paragraph{Affine pre-registration.}
ANTs~\cite{ants} estimates an affine transform from the two CTs, using body masks, a $[-1000,1000]$~HU window, half-resolution images, and Mattes mutual information.
It is converted to a dense displacement field.

\paragraph{Deformable network.}
We use LapIRN~\cite{ref_lapirn} with three pyramid levels at quarter, half, and full resolution.
Each level predicts a stationary velocity field that is added to the upsampled velocity of the coarser level and integrated by scaling and squaring~\cite{ref_arsigny} into $\varphi_\theta$.
Each level receives four channels: the fixed CT and PET and the affinely aligned moving CT and PET.
The levels are trained one after the other; a finer level is initialized from the coarser one, which is first kept frozen and then trained jointly.
Each sub-network has 24 base channels and a trunk of ten residual blocks with an expansion factor of two.

\paragraph{Instance optimization.}
\label{sect:io}
At test time, the deformation of each pair is refined by optimizing a residual stationary velocity field, initialized at zero and integrated by scaling and squaring (seven steps), on top of the total transformation $\varphi$.
The residual is optimized at full resolution with Adam~\cite{ref_adam} (learning rate $0.025$, 40 iterations).
The objective is the same as in training (Sec.~\ref{sect:objective}), with the predicted labels in place of ground-truth labels.
Of the 40 iterates, we return the one with the lowest objective, in which the interpolated (soft) MTV and TLG terms are replaced by their nearest-neighbor (hard) values, i.e.\ by how the evaluation counts them; this selection uses only predicted labels.

\subsection{Registration Objective}
\label{sect:objective}

Network training and IO minimize a weighted sum of four groups of terms (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}):
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{L} = {} &
  \underbrace{\lambda_{\mathrm{NCC}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NCC}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{DSC}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DSC}}}_{\text{registration accuracy}}
  + \underbrace{\lambda_{\mathrm{MTV}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MTV}} + \lambda_{\overline{J}}\mathcal{L}_{\overline{J}} + \lambda_{J}\mathcal{L}_{J} + \lambda_{\mathrm{TLG}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TLG}}}_{\text{PET quantification}} \\
  & + \underbrace{\lambda_{\mathrm{rig}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rig}}}_{\text{bone rigidity}} + \underbrace{\lambda_{\mathrm{smooth}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{smooth}} + \lambda_{\mathrm{NDV}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NDV}}}_{\text{deformation regularization}} .
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
All terms are evaluated on the total transformation $\varphi$.
The PET-preservation terms and the rigidity term are the subject of this study; they can be switched on in network training, in IO, or in both (Sec.~\ref{sect:experiments}).
The two coarse network levels never use them, because lesions and thin bones span only a few voxels at these resolutions.

\paragraph{Registration accuracy.}
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NCC}}$ is the negative local normalized cross-correlation of the CTs, summed over one to three scales depending on the pyramid level.
PET is not used for similarity, because uptake changes between examinations with disease and therapy and a PET similarity would reward matching these changes.
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{DSC}}$ is a soft Dice loss on the CT organ labels.

\paragraph{PET quantification.}
The PET-preservation terms keep the PET measures of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pet_measures} unchanged by the registration.
For the differentiable loss terms, let $M$ be the moving lesion mask and $\widetilde M$, $\widetilde P$ the linearly warped mask and PET.
Nearest-neighbor warping, as used in the evaluation, is piecewise constant in the displacement and gives no useful gradient; linear warping gives a soft lesion boundary instead.
The volume and uptake terms penalize the relative change defined above, using the soft mask,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{MTV}} = \left(\frac{\left|\sum_x \widetilde M(x) - \sum_x M(x)\right|}{\sum_x M(x) + \epsilon}\right)^{2},
  \qquad
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{TLG}} = \frac{\left|\sum_x \widetilde P(x)\widetilde M(x) - \sum_x P_m(x) M(x)\right|}{\sum_x P_m(x) M(x) + \epsilon},
  \label{eq:mtv_tlg}
\end{equation}
with $\epsilon=10^{-5}$.
Two further terms act on $\det J_\varphi(x)$, the local volume scale of the total affine-plus-deformable sampling transform, computed by central differences on the fixed voxel grid.
A determinant of one preserves local volume; values above and below one correspond to expansion and contraction, respectively, of the sampled region in the moving scan (the opposite change in the displayed warped lesion).
Let $W_s$ and $W_h$ be the linearly and nearest-neighbor warped moving lesion masks, respectively, on that grid. Then
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\overline{J}} = \left(\frac{\sum_x W_s(x)\det J_\varphi(x)}{\sum_x W_s(x)+\epsilon} - 1\right)^{2},
  \qquad
  \mathcal{L}_{J} = \frac{\sum_x W_h(x)\bigl(\det J_\varphi(x) - 1\bigr)^{2}}{\sum_x W_h(x)+\epsilon}.
  \label{eq:jac_terms}
\end{equation}
$\mathcal{L}_{\overline{J}}$ constrains the mean volume scale over all lesions, but allows expansion in one region to cancel contraction in another; $\mathcal{L}_{J}$ penalizes these local changes separately.
The mask $W_s$ is held fixed when differentiating $\mathcal{L}_{\overline{J}}$, and the hard mask $W_h$ is not differentiable with respect to displacement; both losses differentiate through the determinant.

\paragraph{Bone rigidity.}
Many PSMA lesions lie in bone, which moves rigidly between examinations.
For each of the 61 skeletal labels of the fixed scan (in IO: the predicted labels), we fit a rigid transformation to the displacement of the structure in closed form~\cite{ref_kabsch,ref_umeyama} and penalize the mean squared residual of its voxels; $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rig}}$ is the mean over structures with at least 50 voxels.
Each bone is fitted independently, so neighboring bones may move relative to each other, and the term involves no finite differences, so it does not constrain the tissue around a bone.

\paragraph{Deformation regularization.}
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{smooth}}$ is the mean squared spatial gradient of the displacement.
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{NDV}}$ is a differentiable version of the challenge's non-diffeomorphic volume, which splits every voxel into tetrahedra and accumulates the negative part of their Jacobian determinants~\cite{Liu2024}.

\subsection{Rigidity Penalties Compared}
\label{sect:rigidity_methods}

Besides the per-bone rigid fit, we evaluate the rigidity penalty of Staring et al.~\cite{ref_rigidity_3_staring}, which requires the Jacobian $J$ inside a bone to be a rotation: $\det J = 1$, $J^{\top}J = I$, and vanishing second derivatives of the deformation.
It is computed with finite differences, whose stencil reads the 6 face and 12 edge neighbors of a voxel.
At a bone boundary, part of the stencil lies outside the bone, so the penalty also acts on the surrounding soft tissue or on a neighboring bone.
A common remedy is to erode the bone mask so that the stencil stays inside; we erode each bone by exactly this 18-neighbor footprint, which removes the leakage completely.
The three variants (Staring, Staring with erosion, per-bone rigid fit) are compared in IO (Sec.~\ref{sect:experiments}).

\subsection{Training}
\label{sect:training}

Each level was trained with Adam~\cite{ref_adam} for $10^5$, $10^5$, and $1.2\cdot10^5$ steps (quarter, half, full resolution) with learning rates of $1.5\cdot10^{-4}$, $1.2\cdot10^{-3}$, and $10^{-5}$, a batch size of one with gradients accumulated over four pairs, a linear warm-up over five epochs, and a cosine decay to 10\,\% of the initial rate.
A finer level is trained jointly with the coarser ones after ten epochs.
Convolutions run in bfloat16, all losses in single precision.
Training took 18~h, 22~h, and 3.7~days for the three levels on one NVIDIA A100 (80~GB).
The augmentations mimic longitudinal variability: left--right flipping ($p=0.5$), CT intensity shifts of $\pm0.02$ in the normalized window (about $\pm50$~HU), CT scaling between 0.9 and 1.1, PET scaling between 0.85 and 1.15, and field-of-view mismatch by cropping up to 40 axial slices from both scans and up to 10 further slices from each scan separately.
The two coarse levels are shared by all models; the variants of this study differ only in the losses of the full-resolution level.
Checkpoints were selected on the validation set: the coarse levels by CT Dice, the full-resolution level by the best accuracy (CT Dice and HD95) among checkpoints whose validation folding stays below $1.4\cdot10^{-4}\,\%$ non-diffeomorphic volume.
We did not select on MTV or TLG, so that the PET losses are not credited with effects of the checkpoint choice.

\paragraph{Use of AI tools.}
Parts of the code for training, evaluation, statistical analysis, and figure generation were written with the help of an AI coding assistant.
All generated code was reviewed and tested by the authors, who are responsible for the analyses and results.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sect:experiments}

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sect:metrics}

All metrics are computed with the provided labels of the test scans by evaluating the full-resolution displacement fields directly.
The moving scan is warped with corner-aligned grid coordinates, and PET and labels are sampled by nearest-neighbor interpolation.
Of the 28 test patients (38 pairs), 26 patients (30 pairs) have lesions in the moving scan and contribute to the PET metrics.

\paragraph{Alignment.}
CT Dice and the 95th percentile of the Hausdorff distance (HD95), averaged over the 117 organ labels.

\paragraph{PET-preservation metrics.}
We compute $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ as defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pet_error}, using the provided moving-scan lesion masks.
``Initial'' denotes the unregistered, unresampled scans; their MTV and TLG change is zero by construction and does not estimate an effect of resampling.

\paragraph{Per-lesion change.}
The aggregate errors of Eq.~\eqref{eq:pet_error} can hide large changes of individual lesions that cancel.
We therefore also split the moving lesion mask into 26-connected components and compute $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ for each.
The 30 pairs with lesions contain 1435 components.
For the per-lesion size and location analysis, we exclude 108 components smaller than five voxels: a one-voxel difference changes their volume by at least 25\,\%, making their relative changes highly quantized.
This leaves 1327 lesions, grouped by size (small $<0.5$~ml, medium $<5$~ml, large) and location (bone-associated if at least half of the lesion lies on a skeletal label).
Scan-level PET metrics and the lesion-coverage analysis retain all 1435 components so that the coverage decomposition includes the full evaluated lesion volume.

\paragraph{Deformation quality.}
Percentage of non-diffeomorphic volume (NDV) as defined by the challenge~\cite{Liu2024}, within the challenge's mask (voxels where the fixed CT exceeds its minimum intensity).

\paragraph{Bone metrics.}
For the rigidity comparison, we additionally compute Dice and HD95 separately over bones and soft-tissue organs.
We measure within-bone distance distortion as the mean relative change in physical distance between randomly sampled pairs of points in the same fixed-scan bone, including its boundary voxels.
We compute this separately for short (5--15~mm) and long ($\geq15$~mm) distances; pairs below 5~mm are excluded because their relative change is sensitive to interpolation and discretization at this image resolution.
Short distances characterize local deformation, whereas long distances characterize deformation across a larger part of the bone.
We also compute NDV inside bones and in a three-voxel band around them and, as a secondary measure, the residual of a rigid fit to each bone's displacement (bone rigidity error).
We also measure the fraction of stencil reads that leave the bone of the center voxel (stencil leakage) and the fraction of bone voxels and of bones that remain after erosion for the eroded Staring penalty.
Erosion does not restrict the point pairs used for distance distortion.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
\label{sect:statistics}

A patient may contribute several pairs and many lesions.
We average each metric over a patient's eligible pairs before analysis: CT Dice, HD95, and NDV comparisons include 28 test patients, whereas MTV and TLG comparisons include the 26 patients with moving-scan lesions.
Paired comparisons use only patients with values for both methods and a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test~\cite{ref_wilcoxon}; the implementation doubles the smaller one-sided $p$-value, drops zero paired differences, assigns average ranks to tied nonzero magnitudes, and returns $p=1$ when all differences are zero.
Holm correction~\cite{ref_holm} is applied separately per metric to three comparisons against ``None'' (no losses) in each ablation block, three IO-only versus training-plus-IO comparisons, four leave-one-out comparisons, and comparisons of our method against the other baseline-table methods (nine for CT, HD95, and NDV; eight for MTV and TLG, because ``Initial'' has zero PET change by construction).
We call a difference significant if its adjusted $p<0.05$.
Confidence intervals are 95\,\% percentile patient-bootstrap intervals~\cite{ref_bootstrap}: scan-level plots show intervals for method means, whereas intervals stated for IO-only minus training-plus-IO are for paired patient-mean differences.
For per-lesion medians, patients are resampled together with all their lesions (cluster bootstrap).

For the comparison with baselines, we additionally rank the nine resampled methods with the significance-based ranking of the challenge: per metric, all 72 directed method pairs are compared with one-sided Wilcoxon tests and Holm correction, and a method scores (number of methods it significantly outperforms $+1$)/$K$ for $K$ methods.
Scores are averaged into accuracy (CT Dice, HD95), PET preservation ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$), and regularity (NDV, after subtracting a practical-equivalence threshold of 0.005\,\% per pair), and combined into $100\cdot\mathrm{acc}^{0.4}\,\mathrm{PET}^{0.4}\,\mathrm{reg}^{0.2}$.
The challenge additionally scores Dice and HD95 of organs with physiological PET uptake, for which no labels are available to us; we omit them.
Dice or HD95 of lesions would not be meaningful either, because lesions appear, disappear, and change between examinations.
The stability of the ranks is shown by 95\,\% rank intervals over 1000 patient bootstrap samples.

\subsection{Tuning and Model Selection}
\label{sect:tuning}

All tuning used the validation set.
For our IO settings and the choice of our method, we use one selection rule: among the candidates of a grid whose CT Dice is within two points of the most accurate one, we keep the one with the lowest $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|+|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$.
The two-point tolerance is a design choice that allows some alignment accuracy to be exchanged for lower PET change; it is not a clinically established threshold.
We do not use the challenge ranking for this selection because it is decided by accuracy: differences in Dice between candidates are consistently significant, whereas differences in MTV and TLG rarely are, so the ranking almost always keeps the most accurate candidate regardless of preservation.
The baselines were tuned with the challenge ranking, ranking the candidates of each method's grid against each other.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{IO:} on the network trained with both losses, step-wise: first learning rate $\times$ iterations ($\{0.0125,0.025,0.05,0.1\}\times\{10,20,40\}$), then a common scale of the four PET weights ($\times\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,1,2,4\}$ of the training-derived values); chosen: learning rate 0.025 with 40 iterations, and $\times0.1$ (Table~\ref{tab:io_tuning}).
  Neither the selection rule nor the ranking rewards rigidity, which costs Dice without reducing the PET metrics or folding, so both would set $\lambda_{\mathrm{rig}}$ to zero.
  We fixed $\lambda_{\mathrm{rig}}=0.25$ so that the selected method retained the bone-rigidity constraint evaluated in this study.
  Its inclusion and numerical weight are therefore design choices rather than outputs of the selection rule.
  \item \textbf{NiftyReg}~\cite{MODAT2010,modat2014}: \texttt{reg\_aladin} followed by \texttt{reg\_f3d} on the GPU with normalized mutual information (local NCC is not available on the GPU), over the bending-energy weights $\{0.0002, 0.0005, 0.001, 0.005, 0.02\}$ and final control-point spacings of $\{3, 5, 7, 9\}$ voxels; chosen: $0.0002$ and 9 voxels.
  \item \textbf{ConvexAdam}~\cite{ref_convexadam}: after its own ANTs affine registration with a $[-300,300]$~HU CT window, over the regularization weight $\lambda\in\{0.1,0.25,0.5,1.25,2,4\}$; chosen: 0.1.
\end{itemize}
Several choices ended at the edge of their grid; extending the grids changed the validation scores only marginally.

\paragraph{Choice of our method.}
Before the experiments, we defined our full method as the network trained with both losses followed by IO with both losses.
Instead, we select our method on the validation set among the IO variants of Sec.~\ref{sect:ablations} and IO directly after the affine pre-registration, with the same rule as for the IO settings: the variant with the lowest $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|+|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ among those within two Dice points of the most accurate one.
This selected the network trained without the losses followed by IO with both losses (79.2\,\% Dice, $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$ 6.9\,\%, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ 8.9\,\%; the most accurate variant, IO without the losses, reached 80.8\,\%).
The variants with the losses in training and IO changed the lesions slightly less (e.g.\ 5.8\,\% and 7.9\,\% with both losses) but were 2.6 points less accurate than the most accurate variant.
We refer to the selected variant as our method and report the predefined full method and IO without the losses alongside it in the comparison with baselines.

\subsection{Comparisons}
\label{sect:ablations}

\paragraph{Effect of the losses.}
Four networks were trained with the same alignment and regularization terms and with neither, the PET-preservation, the rigidity, or both groups of losses.
Each is evaluated as network output and with IO using the same losses as in its training.

\paragraph{Losses in training or only in IO.}
The network trained without the losses is combined with IO using PET preservation, rigidity, or both, and compared with the networks trained with these losses (and IO with the same losses).
To measure what the network contributes, IO with both losses is also run directly after the affine pre-registration.

\paragraph{Comparison with baselines.}
Initial alignment, the affine pre-registration, NiftyReg, ConvexAdam, IO after the affine, and our variants are compared on the test set.
All registrations use the same follow-up-to-baseline pairs and are scored with the same provided organ and lesion labels and original-SUV PET images.
NiftyReg and ConvexAdam use CT alone for registration, with no organ or lesion labels; both remove the patient bed, but each uses its own affine step and CT preprocessing (NiftyReg: \texttt{reg\_aladin} on bed-removed CT; ConvexAdam: ANTs with a $[-300,300]$~HU window).
Our method uses CT and PET, the affine pre-registration described above, provided labels in network training, and predicted labels during IO.
The NiftyReg and ConvexAdam settings were selected on the validation pairs by the challenge ranking, with candidates ranked within each method's grid (Sec.~\ref{sect:tuning}).
Runtimes are per pair on one NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU and include the affine step.

\paragraph{Accuracy--preservation trade-off.}
As a post-hoc test-set sensitivity analysis, IO on the network trained without the losses is run with all four PET weights scaled by $\{0,10^{-4},3\cdot10^{-4},0.001,0.003,0.01,0.03,0.1,0.3,1\}$ relative to the training-derived values and without rigidity.
The factor $\times0.1$ had already been selected from the original validation grid $\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,1,2,4\}$ and is not reselected from this test-set sweep.
Besides the evaluation on the ground-truth labels, we compute CT Dice, $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$, and $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$ on the predicted labels that IO optimizes, with the same definitions.
To probe the effect of lesion-mask quality, we additionally rerun IO with the provided lesion masks instead of the predicted ones (organ labels remain predicted), at the tuned and full PET weights.
This diagnostic run uses labels unavailable in the intended test-time setting.

\paragraph{Lesion coverage in predicted masks.}
Each ground-truth lesion of the moving scan is assigned to a group by the fraction of its voxels covered by the predicted lesion mask: missed (0\,\%), partly covered ($<50\,\%$), or covered ($\geq50\,\%$).
For each group, we report two quantities, both computed per scan as in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pet_error} and averaged over patients: the change within the group, using only the group's lesions, and the contribution of the group to the evaluation metric, i.e.\ the change if only the group's lesions changed and all other lesions kept their volume and uptake.
Since changes of different lesions in a scan partly cancel, the contributions add up to slightly more than the evaluation metric.
\paragraph{Per-lesion terms.}
Because the PET losses constrain the total volume and uptake of a scan, we test whether per-lesion terms can prevent changes in different lesions from canceling.
In the intended test-time setting, per-lesion versions of the volume, uptake, and mean-Jacobian terms are added to the PET terms during IO on the network trained without the losses.
The predicted lesion mask is split into connected components.
Each of the eight largest components with at least 20 voxels gets its own relative volume and uptake change and mean Jacobian determinant, and the terms are averaged over the components weighted by size.
Their weight is selected on the validation set among $\{1,10,100\}$ times a base value scaled like the other PET weights, with the selection rule of Sec.~\ref{sect:tuning} applied to the median per-lesion $|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|+|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$, the quantity these terms target.
As a separate diagnostic, we also apply the terms at their base weight to components of the provided lesion masks, which are unavailable in the intended test-time setting.

\paragraph{Contribution of each PET term.}
On the network trained without the losses, IO with all four PET terms is compared with IO in which one term at a time is switched off.
This test-set comparison is a diagnostic analysis and is not used to select a new loss configuration.

\paragraph{Rigidity penalties.}
This comparison tests whether the penalties preserve bone geometry and whether they provide additional PET preservation beyond the PET-specific terms.
On the network trained with the PET losses, IO with the PET terms is combined with no rigidity, Staring, Staring with erosion, or the per-bone rigid fit.
Because the penalties have different units, a reference weight is first calibrated for each so that its initial value on five validation pairs equals that of the rigid fit at $\lambda_{\mathrm{rig}}=0.25$ (Staring 0.66, Staring with erosion 1.03).
Each method is then evaluated on the validation set at $\{0.1,0.3,1,3,10,30,100\}$ times its reference weight, giving a curve of CT Dice against within-bone distance distortion.
For the test set, we choose for each method the largest weight whose validation Dice is within two Dice points of the variant without rigidity, the same tolerance as in the selection rule above, with the grid capped at $\times100$: $\times3$ for Staring, and $\times100$ for Staring with erosion and the rigid fit.

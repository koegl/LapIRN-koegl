\paragraph{Network training.}
The quarter-, half-, and full-resolution levels were trained with Adam~\cite{ref_adam} for $10^5$, $10^5$, and $1.2\cdot10^5$ steps, respectively, with learning rates of $1.5\cdot10^{-4}$, $1.2\cdot10^{-3}$, and $10^{-5}$.
We used a batch size of one with gradients accumulated over four pairs, a linear warm-up over five epochs, and a cosine decay to 10\,\% of the initial rate.
A finer level was trained jointly with the coarser ones after ten epochs.
Convolutions ran in bfloat16 and all losses in single precision.
Training took 18~h, 22~h, and 3.7~days for the three levels on one NVIDIA A100 (80~GB).
The intensity augmentations were CT shifts of $\pm0.02$ in the normalized window (about $\pm50$~HU), CT scaling between 0.9 and 1.1, and PET scaling between 0.85 and 1.15.
Spatial augmentations were left--right flipping ($p=0.5$) and field-of-view mismatch produced by cropping up to 40 axial slices from both images and up to 10 further slices from each image separately.

\paragraph{Challenge ranking.}
For each metric, all 72 directed pairs among the nine resampled methods were compared with one-sided Wilcoxon tests and Holm correction, and a method scored (number of methods it significantly outperformed $+1$)/$K$ for $K$ methods.
Scores were averaged into accuracy (CT Dice, HD95), PET preservation ($|\Delta\mathrm{MTV}|$, $|\Delta\mathrm{TLG}|$), and regularity (NDV, after subtracting a practical-equivalence threshold of 0.005\,\% per pair).
The composite score was $100\cdot\mathrm{acc}^{0.4}\,\mathrm{PET}^{0.4}\,\mathrm{reg}^{0.2}$.
The challenge additionally scores Dice and HD95 of organs with physiological PET uptake, for which no labels are available to us, so we omitted them.
We also omitted lesion Dice and HD95 because lesions appear, disappear, and change between sessions.
The 95\,\% rank intervals use 1000 patient bootstrap samples.

\paragraph{Tuning grids.}
IO was tuned step-wise on the network trained with both losses: first learning rate $\times$ iterations ($\{0.0125,0.025,0.05,0.1\}\times\{10,20,40\}$), and then a common scale of the four PET weights ($\times\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,1,2,4\}$ of the training-derived values).
The selected setting was a learning rate of 0.025 with 40 iterations and a PET factor of $\times0.1$ (Table~\ref{tab:io_tuning}).
NiftyReg~\cite{MODAT2010,modat2014} used \texttt{reg\_aladin} followed by GPU \texttt{reg\_f3d} with normalized mutual information, because local NCC is unavailable on the GPU.
Its grid combined bending-energy weights 0.0002, 0.0005, 0.001, 0.005, and 0.02 with final control-point spacings of 3, 5, 7, and 9 voxels; the selected setting used 0.0002 and 7 voxels.
ConvexAdam~\cite{ref_convexadam} used its own ANTs affine registration with a $[-300,300]$~HU CT window and regularization weights $\lambda\in\{0.1,0.25,0.5,1.25,2,4\}$; the selected setting used 2.
Several selected settings lay at the edge of their grid, but extending the grids changed the validation scores only marginally.
The post-hoc accuracy--preservation sweep used PET-weight factors $\{0,10^{-4},3\cdot10^{-4},0.001,0.003,0.01,0.03,0.1,0.3,1\}$ relative to the training-derived values.
The per-lesion terms were tested at $\{1,10,100\}$ times base weights of 8 (volume), 8 (uptake), and 15 (mean Jacobian determinant), i.e.\ 0.1 times their initial values, like the other PET weights; the validation set selected $\times10$.
For the rigidity comparison, the Staring and eroded-Staring reference weights were 0.66 and 1.03, respectively, after calibration against the rigid-fit weight of 0.25.
All three penalties were evaluated at $\{0.1,0.3,1,3,10,30,100\}$ times their reference weight; the selected factors were $\times3$ for Staring and $\times100$ for eroded Staring and the rigid fit.

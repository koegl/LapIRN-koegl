# Paper editing checklist for the JMI extension

Read from `main.tex` and its included files, against the six supplied JMI research papers (`024502`, `024503`, `034001`, `034002`, `044002`, `044003`). This is an editorial review of the present draft, not a claim that every feature of the examples is a journal requirement. The source compiles to a 38-page, double-spaced draft; the length includes references and the lists of figures and tables.

**Update after reading `../findings.md`.** Its checked entries describe findings already represented in the manuscript; its unchecked entries describe planned material or a story still to be fully realised. The boxes below are *editing actions*, so they stay unchecked until the corresponding manuscript change is done. In particular, the Q1 → Q2 → leave-one-out → baselines sequence is an agreed outline, not an accidental order to replace. The stencil-leakage measurements are confirmed, while the comparison of rigidity methods is still pending. The predicted-mask limitation is already supported by a test-set decomposition; the ground-truth-mask intervention remains a separate open test.

**What the examples suggest.** All six open with a structured Purpose / Approach / Results / Conclusions abstract, then move from an imaging problem to a specific gap, a testable approach, quantitative results, and a discussion of practical meaning and limitations. They vary in whether Methods and Experiments are separate and whether Discussion and Conclusion are combined. They include disclosures and a code/data availability statement. The useful model here is the *argument*, not a fixed section template. Your draft already has unusually good ingredients for that argument: a clinically motivated measurement problem, a clean training-versus-IO comparison, and an explanation for why preservation has a ceiling.

## Story and claims — settle these first

1. [ ] **#01 — Turn the agreed story into a one-sentence thesis and a visible through-line.** `findings.md:6–39` already sets out the problem, method, Q1 (do the losses work?), Q2 (where should they act?), component analysis, baselines, and two pending experiments. Suggested working thesis: *Lesion-preserving constraints reduce registration-induced PSMA PET measurement change at an anatomical accuracy cost; placement in training or IO depends on runtime, and predicted lesion masks limit what IO can preserve.* Make the title, abstract, end of Introduction, Results transitions, and Conclusion consistently develop that thesis. The present Introduction's two questions and four contributions do not yet give the reader a clear hierarchy (`introduction.tex:18–29`).

2. [ ] **#02 — Give the rigidity comparison a defined supporting role.** The stencil result is already confirmed on 560 scans: 21.2% of stencil reads leave the bone; erosion retains 35.5% of bone voxels and only 2.4% of rib voxels (`findings.md:41–64`). Use this to motivate the per-bone fit, then test whether it improves *measured bone geometry* at an acceptable alignment cost. Keep that mechanism/result distinct from the Q1 PET-preservation finding. Once the validation sweeps and test comparison arrive, decide how much space they merit; `results.tex:77–79` and `discussion.tex:52–53` still have no performance result.

3. [ ] **#03 — Keep the agreed Q1 → Q2 sequence, but make its causal links explicit.** Q1 establishes that the losses work and what accuracy they cost; Q2 asks where to pay that cost given 11 s network versus about 157 s with IO; leave-one-out and per-lesion results explain the mechanism; baselines establish competitiveness; the pending trade-off and rigidity experiments test the boundaries. Open each Results subsection with the question it resolves and close with the one inference needed for the next. The detailed leave-one-out and challenge-ranking material (`results.tex:45–67`) should remain subordinate to this arc rather than becoming separate headlines.

4. [ ] **#04 — Make the clinical use case concrete without claiming demonstrated clinical benefit.** Define the intended downstream comparison (e.g., lesion tracking or response assessment), show why warp-induced changes in the *same* follow-up scan matter, and state what size of error might be consequential if a defensible threshold exists. The study measures registration-induced change, not patient outcome or clinical decision quality (`introduction.tex:4–9`, `methods.tex:163–173`, `discussion.tex:44–45`).

5. [ ] **#05 — Distinguish the journal extension from the Learn2Reg method paper precisely.** Add a compact “prior version versus present study” paragraph or table: dataset/split, retraining, tuned baselines, patient-level inference, IO placement, lesion analysis, and any genuinely new rigidity comparison. Explain what was fixed before the new test evaluation. The current two sentences (`introduction.tex:15–17`) and disclosure that all test patients belonged to the earlier challenge development pool (`methods.tex:15–16`, `discussion.tex:59`) invite questions about independence. Describe the new test set as held out *for this study*, not as fully independent of all prior development.

6. [ ] **#06 — Moderate the central training-versus-IO claim.** A nonsignificant preservation difference does not establish equivalent preservation. Report paired effect estimates and confidence intervals for both MTV and TLG, define an acceptable margin if you want to claim equivalence, or say “we detected no difference” with the present 28-patient uncertainty. Replace “best applied there,” “preserves as well,” and “similar preservation” where they exceed the evidence (`main.tex:44–46`, `introduction.tex:26`, `results.tex:25–27`, `discussion.tex:8–12`, `discussion.tex:65–66`).

7. [ ] **#07 — State what is already demonstrated about the preservation ceiling, then test what remains.** The test-set decomposition already shows that missed lesions (6% of volume) account for 4.8 of 7.6 MTV points and 5.7 of 8.7 TLG points after PET IO (`findings.md:168–175`; `results.tex:90–100`). The earlier validation run with ground-truth masks used a different setup (T4, 20 IO iterations), so it does not replace the planned test-set T1 ground-truth-mask experiment (`findings.md:96–117`). Report that experiment for the covered-lesion remainder (2.6 MTV and 3.2 TLG points). Until then, describe missed lesions as the largest *measured contribution* and avoid claiming segmentation error explains all residual change (`main.tex:44–46`, `discussion.tex:14–20`).

## Study design and technical interpretation

8. [ ] **#08 — Recheck the PET biomarker names with the nuclear-medicine coauthors.** “Total lesion glycolysis” normally names an FDG glucose-metabolism measure, while this study uses PSMA uptake (SUV summed over lesion voxels). If TLG is the challenge's fixed name, say so at first use and consider a parallel clinically accurate term such as total lesion uptake; likewise decide whether “metabolic tumour volume” is the best name for a PSMA lesion-mask volume (`main.tex:40`, `introduction.tex:7`, `methods.tex:163–169`). Keep challenge metric names consistent where they are needed for reproducibility.

9. [ ] **#09 — Clarify exactly what “preservation” measures.** The scan is warped to a different frame, and before/after MTV or TLG is computed on the *moving* scan, so this is induced measurement change, not whether disease burden is correctly tracked over time. Explain whole-scan aggregation, signed-change cancellation, and why per-lesion analysis complements it near the first metric definition (`methods.tex:163–178`).

10. [ ] **#10 — Finish the scorer-control check and sharpen its presentation.** The 2.9 ± 1.2% TLG change under a zero field and the unresampled “Initial” zero are *already described* (`methods.tex:172–173`, `tables/baselines.tex`; `findings.md:66–83`). The notes identify a specific coordinate-normalisation/`align_corners` mismatch in the local copy, so “PET and mask are warped separately” may be an incomplete explanation. Confirm the behaviour against the official scorer before calling it a general resampling floor. Then decide whether a resampled-zero-field control belongs in the main table or supplement, and make the denominator/warping difference clear. Do not treat the unresampled zero as an attainable warped-method target.

11. [ ] **#11 — Move the already documented predicted-label dependency into the main explanation.** The method already states that IO uses predicted masks and evaluation uses supplied labels, and gives the segmentation training/fold setup (`methods.tex:18–25`). Bring that distinction forward before interpreting Q2 and the detection plot; distinguish the 0.51 test-scan lesion Dice from lesion *detection*, *coverage*, and false positives. If validation patients were part of segmentation cross-validation training, explain how out-of-fold predictions protected registration tuning (`findings.md:96–117`).

12. [ ] **#12 — Audit the statistical comparisons and their scope.** State the exact family of hypotheses for each Holm correction, which patients enter PET analyses (26 versus 28), whether confidence intervals are for absolute means or paired differences, and how ties/zero differences enter Wilcoxon tests (`methods.tex:187–199`). The rank bootstrap intervals of 1–5 in `tables/baselines.tex` do not justify declaring a decisive rank winner; frame the rank as descriptive and put the actual metrics first. Also reconcile `findings.md:18–21` with `tables/ablations.tex`: the notes say rigidity significantly lowers *both* PET errors in *both* blocks, but the rigidity-only network+IO TLG value (10.1% versus 11.9%) has no significance mark. Check the statistical output, then use the precise claim in the manuscript and notes.

13. [ ] **#13 — Use a consistent folding description, including in `findings.md`.** Several variants have nonzero NDV, although all are below the challenge's 0.005% threshold (`tables/baselines.tex`, `results.tex:15`). Change “without folding” / “did not fold” to “below the folding threshold on every pair” or give the exact NDV criterion (`main.tex:44`, `discussion.tex:6`, `discussion.tex:50`, `findings.md:30–31`). Also distinguish the test-pair exceedance rate of NiftyReg from patient-level means.

14. [ ] **#14 — Check whether the comparison with baselines supports the intended takeaway.** NiftyReg and ConvexAdam are useful reference methods, but the main claim is the accuracy–preservation trade-off among your own variants. Clarify whether all methods use comparable labels/preprocessing, which affine step each receives, how parameters were selected, and whether baselines were tuned toward the same composite rank (`methods.tex:201–211`, `tables/baselines.tex`). Avoid implying that higher Dice alone makes a variant clinically preferable.

15. [ ] **#15 — Explain the loss definitions in geometric terms and verify the transform convention.** Readers need to know whether `\varphi` is a forward or sampling transform, where its Jacobian is evaluated, why the determinant target is 1, and how nearest-neighbour/linear mask warps affect gradients and evaluation (`methods.tex:35–36`, `methods.tex:78–97`). Check that the interpretation of expansion/compression and the figure's Jacobian color bar match that convention.

16. [ ] **#16 — Separate “rigid bones” from “preserved bone lesions.”** The rigidity penalty fits each fixed-scan bone, whereas the moving lesion mask is warped and may overlap bone only partly (`methods.tex:99–102`, `methods.tex:177–178`). Quantify the bone geometry result when available; a reduction in MTV alone does not prove rigidity, and the bone-associated lesion subgroup does not substitute for a bone-distance measure.

17. [ ] **#17 — State the practical cost of each recommendation.** The network takes about 11 s and IO about 2.5 min per pair, but these are on an RTX A6000 and include the affine step (`methods.tex:234–236`, `results.tex:67`). Tie the recommended operating point to accuracy, MTV/TLG change, and time in one small table or figure. Distinguish batch throughput from latency if relevant to the clinical use case.

## Language, figures, and presentation

18. [ ] **#18 — Repair the abstract after the main claim is fixed.** Its 10.2→6.3% MTV, 11.9→7.2% TLG, and 2.8 Dice-point trade-off compare the *PET-only* IO variants (`tables/ablations.tex`); the 1.7 Dice-point training-versus-IO result compares *PET plus rigidity* (`results.tex:25`, `tables/baselines.tex`). Label those comparisons explicitly or use one coherent operating-point comparison throughout. Add the rigidity *method comparison* only after it exists (`main.tex:39–46`).

19. [ ] **#19 — Correct the qualitative-case inconsistency.** The text calls the displayed registration “our full method” with IO (`results.tex:68–71`), while the caption says “PET and rigidity losses in training, no IO” (`figures/qualitative.tex`). Identify the actual run and align its metrics, caption, discussion, and the corresponding image. A vivid case should illustrate the paper's decision, ideally comparing the same patient with and without preservation constraints, not merely showing good anatomy alignment.

20. [ ] **#20 — Make figure captions readable independently.** Spell out PET, IO, MTV/TLG, NDV, colors, axes, units, and the exact cohort or lesion denominator where needed. For `figures/detection.tex`, distinguish a group's *within-group relative change* from its *contribution to whole-scan error*; for `figures/tradeoff.tex`, make clear that predicted-label and supplied-label curves answer different questions. Ensure color is not the only way to distinguish series in print.

21. [ ] **#21 — Resolve the lesion denominators in one place.** `results.tex:34` and `figures/lesions.tex` use 1327 lesions after excluding components under five voxels; `results.tex:90` and `tables/detection_groups.tex` use 1435 lesions. Say this explicitly in the main text/captions, alongside 26 patients with lesions versus 28 test patients, so the numbers do not look inconsistent.

22. [ ] **#22 — Trim and reorganise the tables for a reader making a decision.** `tables/baselines.tex` has a long caption, 11 columns, significance marks, rank intervals, and a resized table; `tables/ablations.tex` contains six metric columns. Consider a compact main table with 3–4 decisive configurations and core metrics, with full ranking, HD95, NDV, and extensive ablations in supplementary material. Keep uncertainty and sample sizes visible; do not shrink text so far that it becomes hard to read.

23. [ ] **#23 — Reduce method-detail density where it interrupts the argument.** The nine-weight objective table, long training schedule, tuning grids, and challenge rank formula are reproducibility material (`methods.tex:108–145`, `methods.tex:195–211`, `methods.tex:244–247`). Keep enough to understand and reproduce the key comparison, then move parameter grids and secondary derivations to an appendix/supplement if the special issue permits. The manuscript currently takes roughly 16 double-spaced pages to reach Results.

24. [ ] **#24 — Tighten repeated or absolute wording.** “Only way,” “best,” “no remedy,” “none of the metrics,” and “all our variants were more accurate” should have the exact comparison, endpoint, sample, and uncertainty when used (`main.tex:44–46`, `discussion.tex:11`, `discussion.tex:18–19`). Prefer “registration-induced change in MTV/TLG” over unqualified “biomarker preservation,” and use one term for the same concept throughout (follow-up versus moving, supplied versus ground-truth labels, bone-associated versus bone lesions).

25. [ ] **#25 — Make the Discussion interpret rather than replay Results.** Open with the central trade-off and decision rule; then explain the segmentation bottleneck, the reason rigidity helps (if confirmed), the metric/scorer caveats, limits, and clinical next step. The current paragraphs on ablations, ranking, baselines, rigidity, and segmentation partly repeat Results (`discussion.tex:8–53`). Use the conclusion to make one qualified recommendation rather than list all findings again.

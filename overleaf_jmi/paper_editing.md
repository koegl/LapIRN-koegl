# Paper editing checklist for the JMI extension

Read from `main.tex` and its included files, against the six supplied JMI research papers (`024502`, `024503`, `034001`, `034002`, `044002`, `044003`). This is an editorial review of the present draft, not a claim that every feature of the examples is a journal requirement. The source compiles to a 38-page, double-spaced draft; the length includes references and the lists of figures and tables.

**What the examples suggest.** All six open with a structured Purpose / Approach / Results / Conclusions abstract, then move from an imaging problem to a specific gap, a testable approach, quantitative results, and a discussion of practical meaning and limitations. They vary in whether Methods and Experiments are separate and whether Discussion and Conclusion are combined. They include disclosures and a code/data availability statement. The useful model here is the *argument*, not a fixed section template. Your draft already has unusually good ingredients for that argument: a clinically motivated measurement problem, a clean training-versus-IO comparison, and an explanation for why preservation has a ceiling.

## Story and claims — settle these first

1. [ ] **Write the one-sentence claim that governs the paper.** Suggested working version: *For longitudinal whole-body PSMA PET/CT, lesion-preserving constraints trade some anatomical alignment for less registration-induced measurement change; when per-pair refinement is available, the constraints can be applied there, but preservation is limited by which lesions the refinement can see.* Make the title, abstract, end of Introduction, Results order, and Conclusion express this same claim. The current Introduction presents two questions plus four contributions, and the rigidity-penalty comparison threatens to become a second, loosely connected paper (`introduction.tex:18–29`).

2. [ ] **Decide the role of the rigidity comparison once its results arrive.** If it materially strengthens the claim about preserving bone lesions without harming surrounding tissue, make it one supporting result in the main story. If the gain is small or inconclusive, move the detailed stencil/erosion study to an appendix or a separate report and remove it from the abstract/contribution list. Do not promise a result while `results.tex:77–79` and `discussion.tex:52–53` are placeholders.

3. [ ] **Lead Results with the decision a reader has to make.** A possible sequence is: (i) accuracy–preservation trade-off and viable operating points; (ii) training versus IO placement and runtime; (iii) why residual error remains (predicted versus ground-truth masks, then missed/partly covered lesions); (iv) supporting per-lesion and rigidity findings; (v) baselines. The present sequence puts a term-by-term ablation before the reader has seen the main comparison and spends considerable space on challenge ranking (`results.tex:45–67`). Keep detailed ablations as evidence, not the plot of the paper.

4. [ ] **Make the clinical use case concrete without claiming demonstrated clinical benefit.** Define the intended downstream comparison (e.g., lesion tracking or response assessment), show why warp-induced changes in the *same* follow-up scan matter, and state what size of error might be consequential if a defensible threshold exists. The study measures registration-induced change, not patient outcome or clinical decision quality (`introduction.tex:4–9`, `methods.tex:163–173`, `discussion.tex:44–45`).

5. [ ] **Distinguish the journal extension from the Learn2Reg method paper precisely.** Add a compact “prior version versus present study” paragraph or table: dataset/split, retraining, tuned baselines, patient-level inference, IO placement, lesion analysis, and any genuinely new rigidity comparison. Explain what was fixed before the new test evaluation. The current two sentences (`introduction.tex:15–17`) and disclosure that all test patients belonged to the earlier challenge development pool (`methods.tex:15–16`, `discussion.tex:59`) invite questions about independence. Describe the new test set as held out *for this study*, not as fully independent of all prior development.

6. [ ] **Moderate the central training-versus-IO claim.** A nonsignificant preservation difference does not establish equivalent preservation. Report paired effect estimates and confidence intervals for both MTV and TLG, define an acceptable margin if you want to claim equivalence, or say “we detected no difference” with the present 28-patient uncertainty. Replace “best applied there,” “preserves as well,” and “similar preservation” where they exceed the evidence (`main.tex:44–46`, `introduction.tex:26`, `results.tex:25–27`, `discussion.tex:8–12`, `discussion.tex:65–66`).

7. [ ] **Separate observation from causal explanation for the preservation ceiling.** The missed-lesion analysis strongly associates missed masks with residual error, but covered lesions still contribute 2.6 MTV points and 3.2 TLG points (`results.tex:90–100`). Finish the ground-truth-mask IO experiment and report how much error it removes, including the covered-lesion remainder. Until then, soften “remaining change stemmed mainly from missed lesions” and “better segmentation rather than stronger weights is the way” to claims supported by the measured decomposition (`main.tex:44–46`, `discussion.tex:14–20`).

## Study design and technical interpretation

8. [ ] **Recheck the PET biomarker names with the nuclear-medicine coauthors.** “Total lesion glycolysis” normally names an FDG glucose-metabolism measure, while this study uses PSMA uptake (SUV summed over lesion voxels). If TLG is the challenge's fixed name, say so at first use and consider a parallel clinically accurate term such as total lesion uptake; likewise decide whether “metabolic tumour volume” is the best name for a PSMA lesion-mask volume (`main.tex:40`, `introduction.tex:7`, `methods.tex:163–169`). Keep challenge metric names consistent where they are needed for reproducibility.

9. [ ] **Clarify exactly what “preservation” measures.** The scan is warped to a different frame, and before/after MTV or TLG is computed on the *moving* scan, so this is induced measurement change, not whether disease burden is correctly tracked over time. Explain whole-scan aggregation, signed-change cancellation, and why per-lesion analysis complements it near the first metric definition (`methods.tex:163–178`).

10. [ ] **Account for the scorer's TLG resampling floor in every major interpretation.** An identity displacement produces 2.9 ± 1.2% TLG change (`methods.tex:172–173`), yet “Initial” is shown as zero because it is not resampled (`tables/baselines.tex`). Make the differing conditions unmistakable in the table and prose; consider including the resampled identity control alongside the unregistered reference. Avoid presenting zero as an attainable warped-method target.

11. [ ] **Explain the predicted-label dependency and possible bias clearly.** IO optimises organ and lesion *predictions*, while evaluation uses supplied labels (`methods.tex:18–25`). Give the segmentation training population, how validation/test predictions are generated, whether any validation scans used to tune registration were included in segmentation fitting, and the lesion-mask quality by meaningful strata. This dependence is central to the story, not a peripheral implementation detail.

12. [ ] **Audit the statistical comparisons and their scope.** State the exact family of hypotheses for each Holm correction, which patients enter PET analyses (26 versus 28), whether confidence intervals are for absolute means or paired differences, and how ties/zero differences enter Wilcoxon tests (`methods.tex:187–199`). The rank bootstrap intervals of 1–5 in `tables/baselines.tex` do not justify declaring a decisive rank winner; frame the rank as descriptive and put the actual metrics first.

13. [ ] **Use a consistent folding description.** Several variants have nonzero NDV, although all are below the challenge's 0.005% threshold (`tables/baselines.tex`, `results.tex:15`). Change “without folding” / “did not fold” to “below the folding threshold on every pair” or give the exact NDV criterion (`main.tex:44`, `discussion.tex:6`, `discussion.tex:50`). Also distinguish the test-pair exceedance rate of NiftyReg from patient-level means.

14. [ ] **Check whether the comparison with baselines supports the intended takeaway.** NiftyReg and ConvexAdam are useful reference methods, but the main claim is the accuracy–preservation trade-off among your own variants. Clarify whether all methods use comparable labels/preprocessing, which affine step each receives, how parameters were selected, and whether baselines were tuned toward the same composite rank (`methods.tex:201–211`, `tables/baselines.tex`). Avoid implying that higher Dice alone makes a variant clinically preferable.

15. [ ] **Explain the loss definitions in geometric terms and verify the transform convention.** Readers need to know whether `\varphi` is a forward or sampling transform, where its Jacobian is evaluated, why the determinant target is 1, and how nearest-neighbour/linear mask warps affect gradients and evaluation (`methods.tex:35–36`, `methods.tex:78–97`). Check that the interpretation of expansion/compression and the figure's Jacobian color bar match that convention.

16. [ ] **Separate “rigid bones” from “preserved bone lesions.”** The rigidity penalty fits each fixed-scan bone, whereas the moving lesion mask is warped and may overlap bone only partly (`methods.tex:99–102`, `methods.tex:177–178`). Quantify the bone geometry result when available; a reduction in MTV alone does not prove rigidity, and the bone-associated lesion subgroup does not substitute for a bone-distance measure.

17. [ ] **State the practical cost of each recommendation.** The network takes about 11 s and IO about 2.5 min per pair, but these are on an RTX A6000 and include the affine step (`methods.tex:234–236`, `results.tex:67`). Tie the recommended operating point to accuracy, MTV/TLG change, and time in one small table or figure. Distinguish batch throughput from latency if relevant to the clinical use case.

## Language, figures, and presentation

18. [ ] **Repair the abstract after the main claim is fixed.** Its 10.2→6.3% MTV, 11.9→7.2% TLG, and 2.8 Dice-point trade-off compare the *PET-only* IO variants (`tables/ablations.tex`); the 1.7 Dice-point training-versus-IO result compares *PET plus rigidity* (`results.tex:25`, `tables/baselines.tex`). Label those comparisons explicitly or use one coherent operating-point comparison throughout. Add the rigidity result only after it exists (`main.tex:39–46`).

19. [ ] **Correct the qualitative-case inconsistency.** The text calls the displayed registration “our full method” with IO (`results.tex:68–71`), while the caption says “PET and rigidity losses in training, no IO” (`figures/qualitative.tex`). Identify the actual run and align its metrics, caption, discussion, and the corresponding image. A vivid case should illustrate the paper's decision, ideally comparing the same patient with and without preservation constraints, not merely showing good anatomy alignment.

20. [ ] **Make figure captions readable independently.** Spell out PET, IO, MTV/TLG, NDV, colors, axes, units, and the exact cohort or lesion denominator where needed. For `figures/detection.tex`, distinguish a group's *within-group relative change* from its *contribution to whole-scan error*; for `figures/tradeoff.tex`, make clear that predicted-label and supplied-label curves answer different questions. Ensure color is not the only way to distinguish series in print.

21. [ ] **Resolve the lesion denominators in one place.** `results.tex:34` and `figures/lesions.tex` use 1327 lesions after excluding components under five voxels; `results.tex:90` and `tables/detection_groups.tex` use 1435 lesions. Say this explicitly in the main text/captions, alongside 26 patients with lesions versus 28 test patients, so the numbers do not look inconsistent.

22. [ ] **Trim and reorganise the tables for a reader making a decision.** `tables/baselines.tex` has a long caption, 11 columns, significance marks, rank intervals, and a resized table; `tables/ablations.tex` contains six metric columns. Consider a compact main table with 3–4 decisive configurations and core metrics, with full ranking, HD95, NDV, and extensive ablations in supplementary material. Keep uncertainty and sample sizes visible; do not shrink text so far that it becomes hard to read.

23. [ ] **Reduce method-detail density where it interrupts the argument.** The nine-weight objective table, long training schedule, tuning grids, and challenge rank formula are reproducibility material (`methods.tex:108–145`, `methods.tex:195–211`, `methods.tex:244–247`). Keep enough to understand and reproduce the key comparison, then move parameter grids and secondary derivations to an appendix/supplement if the special issue permits. The manuscript currently takes roughly 16 double-spaced pages to reach Results.

24. [ ] **Tighten repeated or absolute wording.** “Only way,” “best,” “no remedy,” “none of the metrics,” and “all our variants were more accurate” should have the exact comparison, endpoint, sample, and uncertainty when used (`main.tex:44–46`, `discussion.tex:11`, `discussion.tex:18–19`). Prefer “registration-induced change in MTV/TLG” over unqualified “biomarker preservation,” and use one term for the same concept throughout (follow-up versus moving, supplied versus ground-truth labels, bone-associated versus bone lesions).

25. [ ] **Make the Discussion interpret rather than replay Results.** Open with the central trade-off and decision rule; then explain the segmentation bottleneck, the reason rigidity helps (if confirmed), the metric/scorer caveats, limits, and clinical next step. The current paragraphs on ablations, ranking, baselines, rigidity, and segmentation partly repeat Results (`discussion.tex:8–53`). Use the conclusion to make one qualified recommendation rather than list all findings again.

## Completion and JMI-facing checks

26. [ ] **Finish the explicitly pending evidence before finalising the abstract.** Complete the rigidity sweeps/test comparison (`results.tex:77–79`), PET-weight trade-off and ground-truth-mask runs (`results.tex:81–85`, `results.tex:100`, `discussion.tex:20`), inspect the final qualitative image (`results.tex:71`), and update the overview to show the IO-only option (`introduction.tex:31–40`). If a run cannot be completed, remove its promised contribution and qualify dependent conclusions.

27. [ ] **Complete availability, funding, and AI-use statements.** Replace the code-link and E.C. funding TODOs (`main.tex:63–75`); specify what will be released (split, training/IO/evaluation code, weights) and any access conditions for data. The six examples consistently include disclosures and code/data availability, though their exact wording varies. Keep the AI statement accurate and aligned with the target issue's current submission policy.

28. [ ] **Remove drafting artefacts and check production layout.** Delete every `\todo` and the helper macro; inspect the final PDF for the long loss-weight table, resized baseline table, crowded figures, and the overfull line in the trade-off grid (`methods.tex:245–248`). Decide whether `\listoffigures` and `\listoftables` are wanted in a submission manuscript; these add three pages here and are not visible as end matter in the supplied published examples (`main.tex:81–82`). Keep line numbering/double spacing according to the active submission instructions, not the published PDF layout.

29. [ ] **Check the special issue's live author instructions at submission time.** The six published PDFs demonstrate common JMI conventions, but they cannot establish the current limits for abstract length, manuscript structure, supplementary files, data statements, or special-issue declarations. Verify those against the issue's author instructions before final upload, and then check the compiled PDF and bibliography one last time.

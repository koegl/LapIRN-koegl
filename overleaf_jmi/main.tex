\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Purpose:} Registering follow-up to baseline PSMA PET/CT scans enables voxel-wise comparison of tumor burden, but the deformation can itself change the metabolic tumor volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG) of lesions.
We examine how PET-preservation and bone-rigidity losses trade alignment for less registration-induced change, and whether they belong in network training or instance optimization (IO).

\textbf{Approach:} We trained LapIRN networks with and without the losses on the Learn2Reg 2026 PSMAReg data, applied them in training, IO, or both, compared three rigidity penalties, and evaluated on 28 held-out patients against tuned NiftyReg and ConvexAdam, with patient-level statistics and per-lesion analyses.

\textbf{Results:} With the losses only in IO, our method reached 78.3\,\% CT Dice (NiftyReg 70.1\,\%, ConvexAdam 61.7\,\%); no difference in MTV or TLG change from either baseline was detected.
\todo{Baseline numbers after re-tuning.}
Without the losses, the mean MTV and TLG changes rose after IO relative to network output; with the losses, IO gained 2.9 Dice points without a detected difference in either scan-level measure.
Applying the losses only in IO gained 1.0 Dice point over applying them in both training and IO; whether preservation differs between these placements remains unresolved.
Lesions missed by the predicted masks contributed more than half of the residual scan-level change in the PET-loss IO analysis.
With provided masks, the scan-level MTV change fell to 0.1\,\%, but individual lesions still changed by a median of 13.5\,\%.
A per-bone rigid fit preserved within-bone distances best (distortion 13.4\,\% to 5.0\,\%).
When added to the PET losses, it produced no detected further reduction in lesion change.

\textbf{Conclusions:} With predicted lesion masks, IO with PET-preservation and bone-rigidity losses improved anatomical alignment without a detected difference in scan-level MTV or TLG change relative to network output.
This option requires time for per-pair refinement; when IO was omitted, training with the losses lowered the mean MTV and TLG changes, with a detected reduction only for MTV.
Missed lesions limit scan-level preservation, while substantial changes to individual lesions remain even with provided masks.
Per-lesion evaluation is therefore needed to judge preservation for lesion-level applications.
\end{abstract}

\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Purpose:} Registering follow-up to baseline PSMA PET/CT scans enables voxel-wise comparison of tumour burden, but the deformation can itself change the metabolic tumour volume (MTV) and total lesion glycolysis (TLG) of lesions.
We examine how PET-preservation and bone-rigidity losses trade anatomical alignment for less registration-induced change, and whether the losses are better applied in network training or in instance optimisation (IO).

\textbf{Approach:} We trained LapIRN networks with and without PET-preservation and bone-rigidity losses on the Learn2Reg 2026 PSMAReg data, applied the losses in training, IO, or both, and compared against tuned NiftyReg and ConvexAdam on 28 held-out patients, with patient-level statistics and a per-lesion analysis. \todo{Rigidity penalties compared.}

\textbf{Results:} Our IO variants were more accurate than NiftyReg and ConvexAdam (CT Dice 75.6--79.2\,\% vs.\ 69.4\,\% and 61.7\,\%), with every pair below the folding threshold.
With IO and no rigidity loss, adding the PET losses lowered the MTV change from 10.2\,\% to 6.3\,\% and the TLG change from 11.9\,\% to 7.2\,\% at a cost of 2.8 Dice points.
Applying both loss groups only in IO gained 1.7 Dice points over using them in training and IO; the paired MTV and TLG differences were $+0.8$ points (95\,\% CI $-1.0$ to 2.7) and $+0.2$ points ($-1.9$ to 2.4), respectively.
IO removed almost all volume change of its predicted lesion masks; missed lesions contributed most of the remaining measured change. \todo{Rigidity result.}

\textbf{Conclusions:} Applying preservation losses only during IO improved alignment over using them in both training and IO, but equivalent PET preservation was not established.
This option requires time for per-pair refinement and predicted lesion masks.
Without IO, training with the losses reduced PET changes.
The benefit of IO depends on whether its masks cover the lesions being measured.
\end{abstract}
